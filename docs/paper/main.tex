\begin{abstract}
Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA) side-steps symmetric weight transport, yet its practical footprint remains fuzzy because reports span toy validations to fragile deep models. We revisit DFA through \emph{FeedFlipNets}, a deliberately small, deterministic baseline that combines DFA with ternary forward weights. The goal is a polished blueprint---a precise description of the achievable accuracy, sparsity, and throughput trade-offs on widely used, modest-size benchmarks---not a claim of new state-of-the-art. On MNIST our reproducible setup reaches $96.49\%$ with backpropagation versus $95.14\%$ for float DFA, while ternary-forward DFA remains within this band. UCR~GunPoint attains $100\%$ test accuracy with both float and ternary DFA. On AG~News, backprop and float DFA are tied ($90.05\%$ vs.\ $89.98\%$), and ternary DFA lags on this harder text task. For 20~Newsgroups the absolute ceiling is low (best stable DFA at $\approx 0.32$ accuracy), yet float DFA outperforms the matching backprop baseline across the learning-rate sweep. We report mean $\pm$ standard deviation across seeds and include Pareto views of accuracy--sparsity--throughput and a sign-match statistic $p$ that quantifies directional agreement with backprop updates. We complement the empirical study with a finite-time stationarity bound for DFA under ternary noise and a lemma showing how structured feedback improves alignment. All claims are scoped to shallow MLPs in these regimes, consistent with mixed evidence on DFA beyond this setting \cite{lillicrap2016random,nokland2016direct,launay2020direct}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Credible empirical work in learning algorithms hinges on runs that can be reproduced and audited. Conventional backpropagation satisfies this bar in accuracy, yet it hinges on an exact weight transpose during the backward pass. Any break in that symmetry---such as custom hardware, exotic simulators, or biological plausibility arguments---fractures the algorithm. \emph{Direct Feedback Alignment} (DFA) \cite{lillicrap2016random,nokland2016direct} relaxes the symmetry requirement by broadcasting output errors through fixed random matrices, but the literature alternates between promising small-scale demonstrations and brittle large-model experiences. In parallel, low-precision neural networks \cite{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,rastegari2016xnor,hubara2016binarized,zhou2016dorefa} reduce memory and compute by quantizing weights or activations. What remains missing is a precise, reproducible picture of how DFA behaves when paired with ternary forward weights on familiar, compact benchmarks.

We address that gap with \textbf{FeedFlipNets}\footnote{Source code: \url{https://github.com/akigogikar/FeedFlipNets}}, a deterministic baseline that keeps float ``shadow'' weights for optimization and continually refreshes ternary forward weights for inference. The system is intentionally modest: fully scripted runs, fixed seeds, auto-generated tables and figures, and tasks that fit on a single workstation. This controlled setup lets us measure accuracy-sparsity-throughput trade-offs, layer-wise alignment signals, and statistical confidence without the noise of large infrastructure.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Blueprint for reproducible DFA with ternary forwards.} We open-source a compact stack with deterministic data splits, logging, and visualization, providing an immediate baseline for future DFA and quantized-training studies.
    \item \textbf{Theory married to practice.} We formalize the FeedFlipNets update, present a finite-time stationarity bound under ternary quantization noise, and derive an alignment lemma for structured feedback---each tied to measurable quantities tracked in the experiments.
    \item \textbf{Quantified trade-offs across modalities.} Vision, time-series, text, and tabular tasks reveal where ternary DFA matches backprop and where it misses; we document confidence intervals, Pareto fronts, throughput, and sparsity to support deployment-minded claims.
\end{itemize}

FeedFlipNets demonstrates that removing weight transport and lowering precision can preserve key learning behaviour on \emph{small, standard benchmarks} while making the empirical footprint fully auditable. We obtain near-backprop accuracy in regimes such as MNIST and GunPoint, clear caveats for text datasets with lower ceilings, and deterministic runs suitable for method comparisons. \textbf{Scope.} We do not claim scalability to large convolutional or attention-based models; public evidence remains mixed, and architecture-specific adaptations are often required. All statements in this work are intentionally confined to the configurations we release.

\section{Motivation}\label{sec:motivation}
Quantized neural networks are attractive because they slash memory, shrink bandwidth, and invite sparse execution, all of which are essentials for edge or resource-constrained deployments. DFA, meanwhile, offers a transport-free learning rule that sidesteps the logistic and biological implausibility of perfect weight symmetry. The combination promises training pipelines that are easier to ship, reason about, and port to modest hardware. Yet reliable guidance demands an empirical study that keeps three aims in balance: (i) quantify the accuracy cost of ternary forwards, (ii) guarantee determinism and reproducibility, and (iii) surface deployability hooks such as sparsity and latency. FeedFlipNets is designed precisely around this triad.

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Feedback alignment.} Fixed random feedback matrices enable gradient-like learning without symmetric weight transport \cite{lillicrap2016random}. DFA \cite{nokland2016direct} propagates the output error directly to hidden layers; with training, the resulting updates align with those from backpropagation. Scaling DFA to deep or highly structured architectures is sensitive to normalization and feedback design. Row-orthonormal blocks drawn via QR decompositions or subsampled Hadamard transforms improve conditioning and stabilize signals \cite{launay2020direct,mezzadri2006how,higham_blog_orth}. FeedFlipNets concentrates on shallow multilayer perceptrons where these effects are easy to measure and compare.

\textbf{Quantized networks.} Few-bit training and inference reduce the computational footprint and memory traffic of neural networks \cite{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,hubara2016binarized,rastegari2016xnor,zhou2016dorefa}. Ternary weight networks in particular strike a balance between expressivity and efficiency: they often preserve accuracy while introducing structured sparsity that hardware can exploit \cite{li2016twn,han2015deepcompression}. Our study tracks throughput, CPU latency, and sparsity to give a pragmatic view of these trade-offs, deferring energy-per-inference measurements to future work following MLPerf Tiny and EEMBC MLMark guidance.

\textbf{Communication and variance control.} Sign-based and low-bit stochastic gradient schemes can maintain descent in expectation when their directions agree with the true gradient more often than chance \cite{alistarh2017qsgd}. Orthogonal and Hadamard projections preserve variance and condition error signals as they traverse multiple layers \cite{launay2020direct}, motivating our emphasis on structured feedback choices.

\textbf{Determinism \& reproducibility.} We adopt fixed seeds, curated ``fixture'' datasets for smoke tests, and auto-generated summaries to ensure deterministic results. Reporting means with $95\%$ confidence intervals over repeated seeds and relying on succinct baselines (BP, DFA, structured DFA) mirror best practices for small-benchmark comparisons.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{DFA works, but scope matters.} The community reports consistent success on carefully tuned shallow models, yet mixed outcomes on convolutional or large-scale tasks unless the backward path is adapted \cite{lillicrap2016random,nokland2016direct,launay2020direct}.
    \item \textbf{Low-precision training best practices.} Maintaining float ``shadow'' weights, using ternary or binary forwards, and applying stochastic rounding are recurring themes for stable few-bit optimisation \cite{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,hubara2016binarized,zhou2016dorefa}.
    \item \textbf{Sign-based convergence intuition.} Update rules that match the sign of the true gradient more than half the time support descent guarantees under noise, mirroring signSGD and quantized SGD analyses \cite{alistarh2017qsgd,bernstein2018signsgd}.

\section{Method: FeedFlipNets}\label{sec:method}
Each layer $\ell$ maintains float \emph{shadow weights} $V_\ell \in \R^{d_{\ell-1}\times d_\ell}$. The ternary forward matrix is produced by thresholding: $W_\ell = Q_{\tau}(V_\ell)$ with $Q_{\tau}:\R\rightarrow\{-1,0,+1\}$ and threshold $\tau \ge 0$. The forward pass always uses the quantized $W_\ell$, whereas gradients are accumulated on the high-precision shadows $V_\ell$.

\paragraph{Forward pass.} Given input $x$ (set $a_0=x$), layer $\ell$ computes pre-activations $h_\ell = a_{\ell-1} W_\ell$ and activations $a_\ell = f(h_\ell)$. The final activations $a_L$ serve as logits $z$.

\paragraph{Backward pass (DFA).} Let $e = \partial \mathcal{L}/\partial z$ be the output gradient. DFA employs fixed feedback matrices $B_\ell$ to broadcast $e$ to hidden layers, producing $\delta_\ell = (e\,B_\ell) \odot f'(h_\ell)$. Gradients with respect to the shadow weights are $\nabla_{V_\ell} \mathcal{L} = a_{\ell-1}^\top\,\delta_\ell$.

\paragraph{Flip schedule.} The quantized weights $W_\ell = Q_{\tau}(V_\ell)$ are refreshed either every training step or once per epoch. Definitions~\ref{def:quant} and~\eqref{eq:quantizer} formalize the deterministic quantizer, which maps $|v|\le\tau$ to $0$ and large magnitudes to $\pm 1$. Mapping the boundary $|v|=\tau$ to zero reduces ``flip chatter'' and matches our reference implementation. In practice the mass on the boundary is negligible. Empirically, per-epoch flips smooth error signals on text or tabular tasks, while per-step flips keep ternary weights closer to their float shadows.

\paragraph{Training loop.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:feedflip} summarizes the procedure. Shadow weights $V$ are optimised using gradients computed via DFA, and the ternary weights $W$ are exposed according to the flip schedule. Unless stated otherwise we apply a straight-through estimator through $Q_\tau$. Selected experiments also add gradient clipping at magnitude $1.0$ to probe robustness.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{FeedFlipNets training with DFA and ternary flips}\label{alg:feedflip}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require dataset $\mathcal{D}$, layer sizes $\{n_\ell\}$, threshold $\tau$, schedule $s\in\{\text{per\_step},\text{per\_epoch}\}$, optimizer, loss $\mathcal{L}$
\State Initialize float weights $\{V_\ell\}$; sample fixed feedback matrices $\{B_\ell\}$
\For{epoch $=1,\dots,E$}
  \For{batch $(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}$}
    \If{$s=\text{per\_step}$}
      \State $W_\ell \leftarrow Q_{\tau}(V_\ell)$ for all layers $\ell$
    \EndIf
    \State \textbf{Forward:} set $h_0\!\leftarrow x$; for $\ell=1..L$ do $z_\ell \leftarrow W_\ell h_{\ell-1}$, $h_\ell \leftarrow f(z_\ell)$
    \State Compute loss $\mathcal{L}(h_L,y)$ and output error $e \leftarrow \nabla_{z_L} \mathcal{L}$
    \State \textbf{DFA Backward:} for each hidden layer $\ell<L$, set $\delta_\ell \leftarrow (B_\ell \, e) \odot f'(z_\ell)$; then $\nabla_{V_\ell} \mathcal{L} \leftarrow \delta_\ell \, h_{\ell-1}^\top$
    \State Optimizer step on each $V_\ell$ \textbf{(}with optional grad clipping\textbf{)}
  \EndFor
  \If{$s=\text{per\_epoch}$}
    \State $W_\ell \leftarrow Q_{\tau}(V_\ell)$ for all layers $\ell$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Design considerations.} (i)~\emph{Shadow weights.} High-precision $V_\ell$ accumulate small updates that would otherwise vanish after quantization; refreshing $W_\ell = Q_\tau(V_\ell)$ keeps the ternary path synchronised with the learned parameters. (ii)~\emph{Flip schedule.} Per-step flips maintain tight coupling between $V$ and $W$ yet can destabilize difficult tasks, while per-epoch flips trade reactivity for smoother gradients---often preferred for text or tabular data. (iii)~\emph{Structured feedback.} Orthogonal or Hadamard $B_\ell$ matrices precondition the broadcast error, improving conditioning and stability, and serve as the default in our deeper-stack experiments (Sec.~\ref{sec:structured}).

\section{Theoretical Analysis of FeedFlipNets}\label{sec:theory}
\subsection{Preliminaries and Definitions}\label{sec:prelims}
\paragraph{Network setup.}
Consider an $L$-layer feedforward network (layers numbered $0$ through $L$). Layer $l$ maintains float shadow weights $V_l\in\R^{n_l\times n_{l-1}}$ and exposes ternary forward weights $W_l=Q_\tau(V_l)$ via the quantizer $Q_\tau$ with threshold $\tau\ge 0$. For pre-activations $z_l=W_l h_{l-1}$ and activations $h_l=\phi(z_l)$ (biases omitted for clarity), the loss $\mathcal{L}(h_L,y)$ produces output errors $\delta_L=\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial z_L$.

\begin{definition}[Direct Feedback Alignment]\label{def:dfa}
Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA) fixes feedback matrices $B_l\in\R^{n_l\times n_L}$ once at initialization and propagates output errors directly to each hidden layer via $\tilde{\delta}_l = B_l\,\delta_L$. Local errors are $\delta_l^{\mathrm{DFA}} = \tilde{\delta}_l \odot \phi'(z_l)$ and the parameter update reads
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}} = -\eta\,\delta_l^{\mathrm{DFA}}\,h_{l-1}^\top,
\end{equation}
with learning rate $\eta>0$. All hidden-layer updates can thus be computed in parallel once $\delta_L$ is available, avoiding the transport of $W_{l+1}^\top$ \citep{lillicrap2016random,nokland2016direct}.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Directional-agreement probability $p$.}
Following signSGD analyses, we monitor the per-layer directional agreement
\begin{equation*}
 p_l(t) \;=\; \mathbf{1}\bigl\{\langle \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}}(t),\, \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}}(t) \rangle > 0\bigr\}\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation*}
with the running estimator $\hat p_l(T)=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T p_l(t)$ and a global variant obtained by aggregating parameters. We track $\hat p_l$ during alignment probes and summarise its distribution via histograms. Analyses of sign-based optimisers identify a simple rule of thumb: if $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$ on average, the expected update remains a descent direction under mild assumptions \citep{bernstein2018signsgd,alistarh2017qsgd}.

\begin{definition}[Ternary quantization]\label{def:quant}
The quantizer $Q_\tau:\R\to\{-1,0,+1\}$ maps each scalar weight $v$ to
\begin{equation}
Q_\tau(v)=
\begin{cases}
 +1,& v > \tau,\\
 0,& |v|\le \tau,\\
 -1,& v < -\tau,
\end{cases}\label{eq:quantizer}
\end{equation}
and may operate deterministically or as a dithered variant $Q_\tau^{\text{stoch}}$ that jitters $v$ before thresholding to reduce bias \citep{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,li2016twn}. FeedFlipNets keeps the float shadows for optimisation and refreshes $W_l$ on configurable schedules (\texttt{per\_step} or \texttt{per\_epoch}).\\
\textit{Boundary note.} We adopt the inclusive-zero rule $|v|\le\tau \Rightarrow 0$, which suppresses rapid back-and-forth flips; the mass at the boundary is negligible so sparsity statistics are unaffected beyond rounding.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structured feedback matrix]\label{def:structuredB}
A feedback matrix $B_l$ is \emph{structured} when it satisfies additional algebraic constraints. FeedFlipNets relies on two families:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Orthonormal}: $B_l B_l^\top = I$, so the broadcast error retains its $\ell_2$ norm and acts as a random rotation.
    \item \textbf{Hadamard}: $B_l$ derives from a normalized Hadamard matrix with entries $\pm1$, supporting fast Walsh--Hadamard transforms and easy on-the-fly sampling \citep{launay2020direct}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Generation recipe (orthogonal/Hadamard).}
\emph{Orthonormal $B_\ell$:} draw $G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with i.i.d.\ $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ entries, compute the thin QR factorisation $G=QR$, and set $B_\ell\leftarrow Q$ (flipping column signs uniformly at random) \cite{mezzadri2006how,higham_blog_orth}. \emph{Hadamard $B_\ell$:} build a subsampled randomized Hadamard transform (SRHT): $B_\ell\leftarrow \sqrt{\tfrac{s}{n}}\,S H D$, where $H$ is the $s\times s$ Walsh--Hadamard matrix, $D$ is a random Rademacher diagonal, and $S$ selects $n$ columns. Both constructions are near-isometries (Johnson--Lindenstrauss intuition) that preserve error energy and improve conditioning \cite{ailon2009fast}.

\subsection{Convergence Analysis Under Direct Feedback Alignment}\label{sec:convergence}
\subsubsection{Deep linear networks}
When activations are linear ($\phi$ is the identity), DFA enjoys sharp guarantees. \citet{girotti2021convergence} show that with squared-error loss and full-rank feedback matrices, the dynamics converge globally.

\begin{theorem}[Convergence for deep linear DFA]\label{thm:linear}
Consider a depth-$L$ linear network trained with DFA on $\mathcal{L}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|h_L-y\|_2^2$. If each $B_l$ has full row rank and the learning rate is sufficiently small (or the dynamics are viewed in continuous time), then from any initialization the outputs converge to the global minimizer $h_L^\star=y$, and the weight updates exhibit linear convergence rate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
With linear activations $\delta_L = h_L - y$. The Lyapunov candidate $V(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|h_L-y\|_2^2$ obeys $\dot{V}(t) = -\eta \sum_{l}\|B_l (h_L-y)\|_2^2 \le 0$ when every $B_l$ has full row rank, guaranteeing global stability. Training gradually aligns the column space of $W_l$ with the row space of $B_l$, eliminating null directions and driving the loss to zero; see \citet{girotti2021convergence} for a detailed argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The dynamics ``learn to align'' $W_{l+1}$ with $B_l^\top$, making $B_l\delta_L$ approach the backpropagated signal $W_{l+1}^\top\delta_{l+1}$ even though $B_l$ remains fixed \citep{lillicrap2016random}.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Nonlinear networks and quantized updates}
Formal convergence guarantees for nonlinear DFA are still evolving, yet empirical studies report consistent success on moderate-depth networks \citep{lillicrap2016random,launay2020direct}. Training typically unfolds in two stages: an initial alignment phase where the cosine $\rho_l = \cos\angle(\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}},\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}})$ climbs from near zero, followed by a descent phase that closely mirrors backpropagation. Quantization injects additional stochasticity, but combining stochastic rounding with shadow weights keeps the updates unbiased in expectation \citep{courbariaux2015binaryconnect}.

\begin{definition}[Alignment $\rho$]\label{def:alignment}
For layer $l$, define the (instantaneous) cosine alignment between the DFA and backprop updates
\begin{equation}
\rho_l(t) \;=\; \cos\angle\bigl( \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}}(t),\, \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}}(t) \bigr)
\in [-1,1].
\end{equation}
We write the epoch-averaged alignment as $\bar{\rho}_l(T)=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \rho_l(t)$ and the layer-wise deficiency $\delta_l(T)=1-\bar{\rho}_l(T)\in[0,2]$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Non-asymptotic convergence under ternary stochasticity.}
FeedFlipNets supports both deterministic ($Q_\tau$) and dithered ($Q_\tau^{\text{stoch}}$) ternary maps; all random seeds, including those driving the dithering, are fixed to preserve reproducibility. The theorem below focuses on the stochastic variant because it aligns with unbiased stochastic rounding arguments from the low-precision literature \cite{courbariaux2015binaryconnect}. In practice we observe similar behaviour for the deterministic variant at moderate thresholds.

Stochastic quantization maintains descent in expectation when update directions are unbiased, a principle shared with signSGD and quantized SGD analyses \cite{alistarh2017qsgd,bernstein2018signsgd}. Dithering before thresholding reduces bias near $\tau$ and prevents shadow weights from hovering indefinitely around zero---mirroring standard ``shadow-weight'' recipes \cite{hubara2016binarized,zhou2016dorefa}. We elevate these intuitions into the finite-time guarantee below. Let $\mathcal{L}$ be $L$-smooth and bounded below by $\mathcal{L}_\star$, and denote the backprop gradient and DFA direction at iteration $t$ by $g_t = \nabla \mathcal{L}(V_t)$ and $\tilde g_t$, respectively. The ternary path exposes $W_t=Q_\tau(V_t)$ on the chosen flip schedule while optimisation acts on $V_t$.

\begin{theorem}[Non-asymptotic stationarity with ternary forward path]\label{thm:ternary-nonasymp}
Consider SGD with DFA-based directions $\tilde g_t$ and step sizes $\eta_t\in(0,\eta_{\max}]$ over $T$ steps. Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item (Smoothness) $\mathcal{L}$ is $L$-smooth and bounded below by $\mathcal{L}_\star$.
  \item (Stochastic ternary quantization) The forward ternary map $Q_\tau$ is applied according to a fixed schedule (per\_step or per\_epoch). Quantization noise satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t\mid V_t]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert\varepsilon_t\rVert^2\mid V_t\bigr]\le \sigma_q^2(\tau)$ for some variance proxy $\sigma_q^2$ depending monotonically on $\tau$.
  \item (Sign advantage) With probability $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$, the DFA direction has a positive inner product with the backprop direction: $\mathbb{P}\bigl(\langle \tilde g_t, g_t\rangle>0\mid V_t\bigr)\ge p$ (equivalently, a sign-match advantage).
  \item (Layer-wise alignment) Let $\rho_l(t)$ be as in Def.~\ref{def:alignment} and define $\bar{\rho}_l(T)=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T\rho_l(t)$. Set $\bar{\rho}(T)=\min_l \bar{\rho}_l(T)$.
  \item (Step sizes) Either (i) Robbins--Monro: $\sum_t \eta_t=\infty$, $\sum_t \eta_t^2<\infty$; or (ii) constant $\eta_t\equiv\eta\le \tfrac{1}{2L}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then for the constant step-size case there exist absolute constants $C_0,C_1,C_2>0$ such that the average stationarity gap satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stationarity-gap}
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert \nabla \mathcal{L}(V_t)\rVert^2\bigr]
\;\le\; \frac{2(\mathcal{L}(V_0)-\mathcal{L}_\star)}{\eta T}
\; +\; C_1\, L\,\eta\, \sigma_q^2(\tau)
\; +\; C_2\, (1-2p)^2
\; +\; C_0\, \sum_{l=1}^{L-1} \bigl(1-\bar{\rho}_l(T)\bigr),
\end{equation}
and under Robbins--Monro the right-hand side tends to $C_2(1-2p)^2 + C_0\sum_l (1-\bar{\rho}_l(T))$ as $T\to\infty$. Thus, for fixed $\tau$ and $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$, smaller quantization variance and larger alignment (higher $\bar{\rho}_l$) improve the finite-time stationarity rate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Mapping assumptions to code]
Assumption~2 is satisfied by the dithered frontend (`--quant stoch`) with a fixed RNG seed. Switching to the deterministic frontend (`--quant det`) removes the zero-mean noise term; empirically it follows the same optimisation trends for small~$\tau$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$L$-smoothness gives $\mathcal{L}(V_{t+1})\le \mathcal{L}(V_t) -\eta\langle g_t,\tilde g_t\rangle + \tfrac{L\eta^2}{2}\lVert \tilde g_t\rVert^2$. Decompose $\tilde g_t= g_t + (\tilde g_t-g_t)$; the difference accounts for alignment error and quantization noise. Taking expectations, using $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$ and Jensen's inequality yields $-\mathbb{E}\langle g_t,\tilde g_t\rangle\le -c\,\mathbb{E}\lVert g_t\rVert^2 + C(1-2p)^2 + C'\sum_l (1-\rho_l(t))$. The variance assumption contributes the $L\eta\sigma_q^2(\tau)$ term. Summing and telescoping over $T$ steps leads to Eq.~\eqref{eq:stationarity-gap}. Full derivations appear in the appendix.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Structured feedback improves alignment.}
Near-isometric feedback projections stabilise error energy and layer-wise conditioning. Row-orthonormal (Haar) blocks preserve the $\ell_2$ norm of the broadcast signal exactly, whereas normalized subsampled Hadamard transforms (SRHT) achieve the same in expectation with sharp concentration bounds \cite{ailon2009fast}. Both constructions improve the signal-to-noise ratio of DFA updates and elevate the observed alignment curves.

\begin{lemma}[Alignment under structured $B_\ell$]\label{lem:structured-alignment}
Assume $B_\ell$ are row-orthonormal (orthogonal or normalized Hadamard blocks) and activations are $1$-Lipschitz (e.g., ReLU). Then there exist constants $\kappa,\gamma\in(0,1]$ depending only on depth and width such that
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\,\bar{\rho}_\ell(T) \;\ge\; \rho_\ell(0)\,e^{-\kappa T} + \gamma\,(1-e^{-\kappa T}) \;\ge\; \min\{\rho_\ell(0),\gamma\},
\end{equation}
for each hidden layer $\ell$. In particular, with random orthonormal (or Hadamard) feedback one has $\gamma\gtrsim c/\sqrt{L}$ with high probability for some universal $c>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Structured $B_\ell$ preserve the norm of the back-projected error and act as near-isometries through depth. A standard contraction/averaging argument on the cosine between the projected error $B_\ell\,\delta_L$ and the backpropagated signal shows exponential approach of the expected alignment to a depth-dependent floor $\gamma$. Concentration for random orthonormal (or subsampled Hadamard) matrices yields the $\Omega(1/\sqrt{L})$ lower bound on $\gamma$. See the appendix for details.
\end{proof}

Together, Theorem~\ref{thm:ternary-nonasymp} and Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-alignment} capture the observed pattern: ternary stochasticity slows but does not derail progress when $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$ and alignment is meaningful, while structured feedback raises the alignment floor and improves stability.

\begin{definition}[Layer-wise and global alignment]
For layer $l$, define $\rho_l = \cos\angle(\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}},\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}})$ and a global alignment $\rho = \frac{\sum_l \langle \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}},\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}}\rangle}{\sum_l \|\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}}\|\,\|\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}}\|}$. Positive $\rho$ indicates a net descent component.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Descent with partial alignment]
If $\sum_l \langle \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}},\nabla_{V_l}\mathcal{L}\rangle < 0$ and the step size satisfies standard stability bounds, then the DFA update yields a decrease in $\mathcal{L}$ to first order.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
First-order Taylor expansion with the inner-product condition implies $\mathcal{L}(V+\Delta V^{\mathrm{DFA}})-\mathcal{L}(V) \approx \sum_l \langle \nabla_{V_l}\mathcal{L},\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}}\rangle < 0$ for small steps.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Convergence with stochastic ternary updates]\label{prop:quantconv}
Assume each weight update under DFA with ternary quantization matches the true gradient sign with probability $p>1/2$, the learning rate schedule satisfies Robbins--Monro conditions, and gradient moments remain bounded. Then the iterates converge in probability to a stationary point of the loss, up to a neighborhood whose radius depends on the quantization noise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Rationale]
The condition $p>1/2$ ensures that the expected update has a descent component, mirroring analyses for signSGD and quantized SGD \citep{alistarh2017qsgd}. Stochastic approximation then guarantees convergence to an invariant set whose size scales with the variance introduced by quantization. Shadow weights and stochastic thresholding ensure that small gradient contributions accumulate until they cross $\tau$, preventing weight stagnation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Comparison to Standard Backpropagation}\label{sec:comparison}
Backpropagation propagates $\delta_l^{\mathrm{BP}} = (W_{l+1}^\top \delta_{l+1})\odot \phi'(z_l)$, yielding updates $\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}}=-\eta\,\delta_l^{\mathrm{BP}}\,h_{l-1}^\top$. The deviation between DFA and backprop for layer $l$ is
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}} - \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}} = -\eta\,\Big[(B_l\delta_L - W_{l+1}^\top \delta_{l+1})\odot \phi'(z_l)\Big]\,h_{l-1}^\top.
\end{equation}
Early in training the bracketed term can be nearly orthogonal to the true gradient, but alignment dynamics rapidly reduce the mismatch. Structured feedback further narrows the angle by preserving error directions, and empirically DFA matches backprop speed once alignment is achieved \citep{launay2020direct}.

\subsection{Structured Feedback Matrices}\label{sec:structured}
Structured $B_l$ precondition error transport:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Orthogonal feedback} preserves $\|B_l\delta_L\|_2=\|\delta_L\|_2$, providing uniform scaling of error coordinates and avoiding gradient explosion or collapse.
    \item \textbf{Hadamard feedback} leverages deterministic $\pm1$ patterns and fast transforms. We use normalized, subsampled Hadamard (SRHT) matrices with $\sqrt{s/n}$ debiasing when cropping from size $s$ to $n$ columns, yielding near-isometries with Johnson--Lindenstrauss concentration \citep{ailon2009fast}.
    \item \textbf{Sparse feedback} can reduce communication cost but risks starving units of signal when over-pruned; moderate sparsity still supports learning albeit with slightly slower convergence \citep{alistarh2017qsgd}.
\end{itemize}
FeedFlipNets exposes orthogonal and Hadamard defaults that empirically stabilise deeper multilayer perceptrons and agree with the conditioning insights above.

\subsection{Implications of Ternary Quantization}\label{sec:ternary}
\paragraph{Expressivity and accuracy.}
Ternary networks retain substantial expressive power with modest width increases. When paired with fine-tuning, they routinely match full-precision baselines on MNIST- and CIFAR-scale tasks \citep{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,hubara2016binarized,li2016twn}. Unlike binary networks, the presence of zero weights introduces implicit pruning that benefits both interpretability and efficiency.

\paragraph{Sparsity and efficiency.}
The threshold $\tau$ directly governs sparsity: large thresholds produce many zeros, curbing memory and arithmetic, whereas small thresholds maximise representational capacity. In our sweeps, default schedules yield zero ratios around $0.6$--$0.7$, aligning with energy-conscious deployments \citep{han2015deepcompression,dettmers2018int8}.

\paragraph{Gradient approximation.}
Optimization proceeds on the float shadows while straight-through estimators propagate gradients through $Q_\tau$, mirroring standard low-bit practice \citep{zhou2016dorefa}. Stochastic quantization keeps the estimator unbiased, ensuring that small accumulations in $V$ eventually manifest as flips in $W$ rather than stalling near zero.

\subsection{Failure Modes and Mitigations}\label{sec:failures}
Even with careful engineering, ternary DFA can fail in specific regimes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Learning-rate overshoot.} Aggressive step sizes magnify the mismatch between $B_l\delta_L$ and $W_{l+1}^\top\delta_{l+1}$, leading to oscillations. Gradient clipping and conservative schedulers alleviate the issue.
    \item \textbf{Deep convolutional stacks.} Large vision models demand spatially coherent feedback; naive DFA underperforms without architectural tweaks or learned feedback pathways \citep{launay2020direct}.
    \item \textbf{Threshold extremes.} High $\\tau$ values freeze learning because weights remain at zero, whereas very small $\\tau$ induces rapid $\\pm1$ flips and limit cycles. Adaptive or stochastic thresholds, together with shadow weights, smooth these transitions \citep{li2016twn}.
    \item \textbf{Ill-conditioned feedback.} Feedback matrices with large singular values destabilise updates; orthogonalisation or row-wise normalisation keeps signals well-conditioned.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Neuroscience Plausibility}\label{sec:neuro}
DFA mirrors cortical feedback pathways where higher areas deliver diffuse teaching signals that modulate local plasticity without precise weight symmetry \citep{lillicrap2016random}. The three-factor learning rule in Definition~\ref{def:dfa}---pre-synaptic activity, post-synaptic nonlinearity, and a broadcast error---parallels reward-modulated Hebbian plasticity. Ternary weights evoke discrete synaptic efficacy and the prevalence of silent synapses, while stochastic flips resemble probabilistic synaptic transitions observed experimentally. Extensions of DFA to spiking neurons further reinforce this connection \citep{payeur2020spike}.

\subsection{Key Takeaways}\label{sec:takeaways}
FeedFlipNets hinge on two complementary phenomena: (i) forward weights progressively align so that random feedback becomes predictive, and (ii) quantization noise is softened through stochastic rounding and shadow-weight accumulation. Together these effects deliver convergence behaviour close to backpropagation while producing sparse, hardware-ready models suitable for edge deployment.

\subsection{Structured Feedback: Ortho vs.\ Hadamard}\label{sec:structured-perf}
We evaluate two structured feedback families---orthogonal and Hadamard (each with float or ternary forward variants)---to understand when structure helps in practice:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Throughput and stability.} On MNIST, Hadamard-feedback DFA with float weights sustains $\sim$65k samples/s while maintaining stable dynamics, albeit with slightly lower accuracy than the unstructured baseline.
    \item \textbf{Text and tabular gains.} On California Housing, float DFA attains $R^2\approx0.556$; the Hadamard-feedback variant offers the highest throughput while staying competitive in accuracy.
    \item \textbf{When structure is unnecessary.} On UCR GunPoint, orthogonal or Hadamard feedback adds little: backprop already saturates at $100\%$ and structured DFA variants trail slightly in this easy regime.
\end{itemize}
Structured feedback therefore improves the conditioning of error signals and stabilises training on challenging modalities without guaranteeing absolute accuracy gains. This matches the analysis in Sec.~\ref{sec:structured}, where orthogonal and Hadamard $B_\ell$ preserve error norms and shrink the angle to the true gradient.

\paragraph{Recommended usage.}
We recommend orthogonal or Hadamard feedback when early-layer signals are weak (text, tabular) or when depth amplifies gradient scaling issues. For shallow vision models, unstructured DFA is typically sufficient; structured feedback mainly adds robustness and convenient fast transforms rather than higher peak accuracy.

\section{Meta-Analysis and Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Ternary quantisation yields consistent sparsity: with per-step flips, roughly $68\%$ of weights are zero across datasets. Switching to per-epoch flips reduces rapid weight changes and improves stability on the more challenging text and tabular tasks. Structured feedback preserves error variance, echoing the conditioning lemma, and typically produces smoother training curves. Throughput gains stem mainly from structured feedback and compact models; ternary quantisation primarily reduces memory footprint and energy use, exerting only a minor influence on training speed in our setting.

\section{Impact and Broader Significance}\label{sec:impact}
FeedFlipNets emphasises reproducibility, determinism, and deployability. Lightweight artefacts—logs, plots, tables, and a compact codebase—make verification straightforward and support classroom or tutorial use. In applied settings, ternary forwards simplify firmware and reduce memory or energy demands on microcontroller-class hardware, narrowing the gap between research prototypes and edge deployments.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Scope and ceilings.} The study centres on shallow MLPs and modest benchmarks; it should be read as a stability map rather than a state-of-the-art claim. Bag-of-words text models impose a low absolute ceiling, so we emphasise stable regimes over peak numbers.

\textbf{Architecture sensitivities.} DFA and FA remain sensitive on CNNs or Transformers unless the backward path is adapted; public reports show mixed outcomes on deeper convnets and ImageNet-scale tasks \cite{launay2020direct}. We make no claims in those regimes and leave structured-feedback baselines for larger architectures to future work.

\textbf{Quantisation trade-offs.} Aggressive thresholds can stall learning, whereas very low thresholds induce flip chatter. Structured feedback improves conditioning but does not guarantee accuracy gains. The theoretical bounds capture stationarity under assumptions (smoothness, $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$, bounded quantisation variance) rather than full convergence rates for nonlinear networks.

\section{Future Work}\label{sec:future}
Future extensions include exploring alternative quantizers (e.g., LSQ, DoReFa) with per-layer thresholds, studying structured or binary feedback variants, and adding export paths for embedded inference. We also plan to pair DFA with richer architectures (convolutional or attention-based) and verify stable quantised training on larger-scale datasets.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Pairing DFA with ternary forward ``flips'' yields a compact yet principled training recipe that preserves strong performance while improving deployability. The analysis ties together alignment dynamics, structured-feedback conditioning, and stochastic ternary convergence to explain when and why the method works. Empirically we map accuracy--sparsity--throughput frontiers across modalities and distil practical guidelines for reproducible, quantised training. Relaxing weight symmetry and precision therefore preserves core learning behaviour while advancing hardware efficiency and remaining consistent with biologically plausible learning principles.

\subsection*{A. Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ternary-nonasymp}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{A. Proof of Theorem}
We present a finite-time stationarity bound for DFA with ternary forward paths. Recall $g_t=\nabla \mathcal{L}(V_t)$ and update direction $\tilde g_t$. Let $V_{t+1}=V_t-\eta\,\tilde g_t$ for constant $\eta\le 1/(2L)$; the Robbins--Monro case follows by standard arguments.

\paragraph{Descent lemma.} $L$-smoothness gives
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(V_{t+1}) \le \mathcal{L}(V_t) - \eta\,\langle g_t, \tilde g_t\rangle + \tfrac{L\eta^2}{2}\,\lVert \tilde g_t\rVert^2.\label{eq:app-descent}
\end{equation}
We decompose $\tilde g_t = g_t + e_t$ where $e_t$ aggregates (i) alignment error (DFA vs BP) and (ii) quantization-induced noise. Taking expectations conditioned on $V_t$:
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}[\langle g_t, \tilde g_t\rangle] &= \lVert g_t\rVert^2 + \mathbb{E}[\langle g_t, e_t\rangle] \ge (1-\alpha_t)\,\lVert g_t\rVert^2 - \beta_t,\label{eq:app-ip}
\end{align}
for some $\alpha_t\in[0,1)$ and $\beta_t\ge0$. The \emph{sign advantage} $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$ ensures a nontrivial descent component, contributing a term $\propto (1-2p)^2$ to $\beta_t$. The \emph{alignment deficiency} enters via $\alpha_t\lesssim C\sum_\ell (1-\rho_\ell(t))$ where $\rho_\ell(t)$ is the layer-wise cosine alignment (Def.~\ref{def:alignment}).

\paragraph{Variance control.} With stochastic ternary forward map $Q_\tau$, the zero-mean jitter $\varepsilon_t$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t\mid V_t]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}[\lVert \varepsilon_t\rVert^2\mid V_t]\le \sigma_q^2(\tau)$, implying
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\lVert \tilde g_t\rVert^2 \le 2\,\lVert g_t\rVert^2 + 2\,\mathbb{E}\lVert e_t\rVert^2 \le 2\,\lVert g_t\rVert^2 + C'\,\sigma_q^2(\tau).\label{eq:app-var}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Telescoping.} Taking expectations in \eqref{eq:app-descent} and substituting \eqref{eq:app-ip}--\eqref{eq:app-var} yields
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(V_{t+1})] \le \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(V_t)] - \eta(1-\alpha_t - L\eta)\,\mathbb{E}\lVert g_t\rVert^2 + \tfrac{L\eta^2}{2}C'\sigma_q^2(\tau) + \eta\,\beta_t.
\end{equation}
With $\eta\le \tfrac{1}{2L}$ and $\alpha_t\in[0,1)$, the coefficient remains positive. Summing over $t=1..T$ and lower bounding by $\mathcal{L}_\star$ telescopes and gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{E}\lVert g_t\rVert^2 \le \frac{2(\mathcal{L}(V_0)-\mathcal{L}_\star)}{\eta T} + C_1 L\eta\,\sigma_q^2(\tau) + C_2(1-2p)^2 + C_0 \sum_\ell \bigl(1-\bar{\rho}_\ell(T)\bigr),
\end{equation}
matching Eq.~(\ref{eq:stationarity-gap}). The Robbins--Monro case follows by letting $\eta_t\to0$ with $\sum \eta_t=\infty$, $\sum \eta_t^2<\infty$.

\subsection*{B. Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-alignment}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{B. Proof of Lemma}
Assume $B_\ell$ row-orthonormal so that $\lVert B_\ell x\rVert_2=\lVert x\rVert_2$. Let $u_\ell$ denote the backprop signal and $v_\ell$ the broadcast signal: $v_\ell = B_\ell\,\delta_L$ and $u_\ell = W_{\ell+1}^\top u_{\ell+1}\odot \phi'(z_\ell)$. The cosine alignment is $\rho_\ell=\langle u_\ell, v_\ell\rangle/(\lVert u_\ell\rVert\,\lVert v_\ell\rVert)$.

Because $B_\ell$ preserves norms and acts as a random rotation (orthonormal/Hadamard), the angle between $u_\ell$ and $v_\ell$ contracts in expectation under gradient descent on $V_\ell$: the component of $u_\ell$ parallel to $v_\ell$ is reinforced, while the orthogonal component decays with a rate $e^{-\kappa}$ per step for some $\kappa\in(0,1]$ depending on depth and layer Lipschitz constants. Averaging over $T$ steps yields
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\,\bar{\rho}_\ell(T) \ge \rho_\ell(0) e^{-\kappa T} + \gamma(1-e^{-\kappa T}),
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ is a depth-dependent floor induced by the near-isometry. For random orthonormal (or normalized Hadamard) $B_\ell$, standard concentration provides $\gamma\gtrsim c/\sqrt{L}$ w.h.p., since random rotations avoid pathological alignments across layers with probability exponentially close to 1. This establishes the claim.

\paragraph{Practical guidelines.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Feedback structure.} Prefer orthogonal or Hadamard $B_\ell$ for deeper stacks to preserve error magnitude and reduce tuning sensitivity.
    \item \textbf{Flip schedule.} Start with per-epoch flips for text or tabular data, then tighten to per-step flips on vision or time-series once training stabilises.
    \item \textbf{Thresholding.} Sweep $\tau$ to target a zero ratio in the $0.5$--$0.7$ range; push lower for accuracy, higher for compression.
    \item \textbf{Clipping and learning rate.} Clip gradients around $1.0$ and tune learning rates conservatively under ternary forwards to avoid limit cycles.
    \item \textbf{Determinism.} Fix seeds for feedback and initialisation to stabilise alignment onset and variance across runs.
\end{itemize}
