\begin{abstract}
\textbf{FeedFlipNets} trains ternary networks by using a cheap feedback signal to \emph{flip bits directly}: weights are stored only as ternary values $\{-1,0,+1\}$ plus a small integer flip-accumulator---\emph{no float shadow weights}---and a per-weight vote flips a bit when enough evidence accrues. Because a flip needs only the \emph{sign} of ``should this weight go up or down,'' the natural feedback is Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA): it broadcasts the output error transport-free, with no backward lock and no shadow storage. We make three contributions. (i) The bit-flip mechanism is genuinely efficient: with an accurate sign it matches float-shadow backpropagation at $\approx\!5.6\times$ less state and $\approx\!2\times$ faster steps. (ii) To supply a better transport-free sign we introduce \textbf{Activation-Routed DFA} (\ARDFA), which reuses the parts of a Transformer block's Jacobian that are already cached in the forward pass---the softmax routing matrix, its Jacobian, the LayerNorm scale, the nonlinearity mask---and surrogates \emph{only} the weight-transposes; this makes the value-path gradients \emph{exact} (matching autograd to $\sim\!10^{-15}$) and, with a perturbation-taught surrogate, cuts the worst-case attention-block \emph{cosine}-alignment angle from $\approx\!90^{\circ}$ to $46.4^{\circ}$, all lock-free. (iii) The central, and negative, finding: this alignment gain does \emph{not} help bit-flipping. On a ternary Transformer, transport-free flipping is worse than not flipping at all, and \ARDFA{} does not beat vanilla DFA---because per-weight \emph{sign}-match to the true gradient stays near chance ($p\!\approx\!0.53$) even as the \emph{cosine} alignment improves sharply. Aggregate alignment is the wrong proxy: the binding constraint for transport-free discrete/ternary learning is per-weight \emph{sign correctness}. We support this with pre-registered gates across depth, attention, accuracy, per-step cost, an analytical pipeline model, and a transport-fraction frontier; every guard and number is reproducible.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Backpropagation (BP) computes exact gradients but pays for it structurally: the backward pass is a sequential chain that transports the transpose of each weight matrix and cannot begin at a layer until the layer above has finished. \emph{Direct Feedback Alignment} (DFA) \citep{lillicrap2016random,nokland2016direct} removes weight transport by broadcasting the output error $\delta_L$ directly to each hidden layer $\ell$ through a fixed matrix $B_\ell$. This makes every layer's update depend only on the shared broadcast error---the backward ``lock'' is gone, and updates can proceed in parallel. That property is the entire reason to prefer DFA: it is the one axis on which a transport-free method could plausibly beat BP (parallel, low-communication training on the right hardware).

DFA's difficulty is equally well known: it trades exactness for a random surrogate, and the surrogate degrades where models are largest. The alignment between the DFA update and the true gradient decays with depth \citep{bartunov2018assessing,refinetti2021align}, and it is especially poor on self-attention, whose effective Jacobian depends on the input and therefore \emph{moves every forward pass}, so a single fixed $B_\ell$ cannot track it \citep{launay2020direct}. The natural question is whether a better---but still transport-free---feedback rule can close enough of this alignment gap to make DFA worth its promise.

This paper answers that question in two parts. First, we show that a large part of the alignment gap on attention is closable transport-free, by observing that a Transformer block's backward pass factors into two kinds of maps. \emph{Weight maps} (the projection transposes $W_Q^\top,W_K^\top,W_V^\top,W_O^\top,W_1^\top,W_2^\top$) are exactly the weight transport DFA forbids. \emph{Data maps}---the softmax routing matrix $A=\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d})$, its Jacobian, the LayerNorm scale, and the nonlinearity mask---are functions of the activations, are \emph{already cached in the forward pass}, and reusing them is therefore \emph{not} weight transport. \textbf{Activation-Routed DFA} (\ARDFA) reuses the data maps exactly and approximates only the weight transposes. Two consequences fall out cleanly: the value/output-projection gradients become \emph{exact} at zero transport cost, and---when the residual surrogate is adapted by a transport-free perturbation rule---the worst-case attention-block alignment angle drops from $\approx\!90^{\circ}$ to $46.4^{\circ}$.

Second, we put the mechanism to work in the setting that motivates the project: \textbf{FeedFlip}, a bit-flip training rule for ternary networks. Weights are stored only as ternary values plus a small integer flip-accumulator---no float shadow weights---and each step casts a per-weight vote in the descent direction, flipping a bit once the accumulated evidence crosses a threshold. A flip consumes only the \emph{sign} of the update, so DFA's cheap, transport-free, shadow-free signal is the natural feedback, and the state per weight collapses from a $32$-bit float shadow to a few bits. With an accurate sign this mechanism is efficient and effective: it matches float-shadow BP at $\approx\!5.6\times$ less state and $\approx\!2\times$ faster steps.

Third, and this is the paper's main scientific message, we treat ``does the alignment gain help?'' as a falsifiable question with pre-registered gates, and report the answer honestly: \textbf{it does not.} On a ternary Transformer, transport-free bit-flipping is \emph{worse than not flipping at all}, and \ARDFA{} does not beat vanilla DFA. The reason is precise and, we think, the useful lesson of the paper: a bit-flip needs the per-weight \emph{sign} of the gradient, and both DFA and \ARDFA{} match that sign only near chance ($p\!\approx\!0.53$)---\emph{even as} \ARDFA{}'s aggregate \emph{cosine} alignment improves from $90^{\circ}$ to $46^{\circ}$. Cosine alignment---the standard DFA metric---is thus the wrong proxy for what discrete, transport-free learning actually needs. We corroborate the same separation on the continuous side (the alignment gain does not make DFA competitive on accuracy, per-step cost, or wall-clock-to-target either), so the binding constraint on transport-free learning is not aggregate alignment but per-weight sign correctness.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{FeedFlip} (Sec.~\ref{sec:feedflip}): a shadow-free, sign-driven bit-flip training rule for ternary networks. Weights are ternary plus a small integer accumulator; with an accurate sign it matches float-shadow BP at $\approx\!5.6\times$ less state and $\approx\!2\times$ faster steps.
  \item \textbf{Activation-Routed DFA} (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}): a transport-free feedback rule that reuses a Transformer block's cached data-maps and surrogates only the weight transposes, yielding \emph{value-path-exact} gradients (Prop.~\ref{prop:vpe}) and a large improvement in attention \emph{cosine} alignment---our best attempt at a good transport-free sign.
  \item \textbf{The per-weight sign barrier} (Secs.~\ref{sec:feedflip}--\ref{sec:gap}): the central finding. Transport-free flipping fails on a ternary Transformer and \ARDFA{} does not rescue it, because per-weight sign-match stays near chance \emph{even as} cosine alignment improves sharply. Cosine alignment is the wrong proxy; per-weight sign correctness is the binding constraint. We corroborate the same separation on continuous training (accuracy, per-step cost, wall-clock-to-target) via pre-registered gates; every guard and number is reproducible.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Feedback alignment.} Feedback alignment \citep{lillicrap2016random} replaces the transposed forward weights in the backward pass with fixed random matrices; DFA \citep{nokland2016direct} broadcasts the output error directly to each hidden layer. Over training the forward weights partially align to the feedback matrices, so the random update acquires a descent component \citep{refinetti2021align}. Scaling studies find the approach competitive on shallow networks but increasingly far from BP as depth and task difficulty grow \citep{bartunov2018assessing}, with attention-based and convolutional models the hardest cases \citep{launay2020direct}.

\paragraph{Learning the feedback.} A separate line closes part of the gap by \emph{learning} the feedback to track $W^\top$: weight mirrors and the Kolen--Pollack rule \citep{akrout2019deep}, and target-projection variants such as DRTP \citep{frenkel2021learning}. Methods that recover BP essentially exactly---predictive coding and target propagation \citep{whittington2017approximation}---do so through iterative, layer-local settling that reintroduces a sequential dependency, i.e.\ the very cost DFA removes. \ARDFA{} occupies a different point: it keeps the fixed broadcast across layers (preserving the lock-free property) and instead recovers exactness \emph{within} a block by reusing cached data-maps, learning only a small residual surrogate.

\paragraph{Removing the backward lock.} Synthetic gradients / decoupled neural interfaces \citep{jaderberg2017decoupled} and decoupled greedy learning also break update-locking, at the cost of auxiliary models or local objectives. DFA is a zero-extra-parameter point in this space; our cost model (Sec.~\ref{sec:econ}) borrows the pipeline-bubble and activation-memory accounting from GPipe and 1F1B/PipeDream \citep{huang2019gpipe,narayanan2019pipedream} to quantify the wall-clock consequences.

\paragraph{Ternary and discrete training.} Ternary and binary networks quantize weights to $\{-1,0,+1\}$ or $\{\pm1\}$ and are typically trained with a float \emph{shadow} weight and a straight-through estimator \citep{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,li2016twn}; sign-based optimizers such as signSGD \citep{bernstein2018signsgd} show that the gradient \emph{sign} alone can drive learning. FeedFlip combines these observations---discrete weights and sign-only updates---and removes the float shadow entirely, storing only the ternary value and a small flip-accumulator. An earlier version of this project studied deterministic DFA with ternary \emph{shadow}-weight forwards as a stability baseline on small standard benchmarks; that shadow-weight variant is distinct from the shadow-free bit-flip rule here and is summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:ternary}.

\section{Method: Activation-Routed DFA}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Setup and the transport-free invariant}
We study a pre-LayerNorm, single-head decoder block. With residual input $x$,
\begin{align}
y &= \mathrm{LN}(x), \quad Q=yW_Q,\; K=yW_K,\; V=yW_V, \label{eq:qkv}\\
A &= \mathrm{softmax}\!\big(QK^\top/\sqrt{d}\big),\quad \mathrm{Ctx}=AV,\quad x_1 = x + \mathrm{Ctx}\,W_O, \label{eq:attn}\\
x_2 &= x_1 + W_2\,\phi(W_1\,\mathrm{LN}(x_1)), \label{eq:mlp}
\end{align}
with a causal mask applied inside the softmax for language modeling. A stack of such blocks maps token embeddings to logits.

\begin{definition}[Lock-free feedback]\label{def:lockfree}
A feedback rule is \emph{lock-free} if, inside the computation of any parameter gradient, the only tensors that cross a layer boundary are (i) the shared broadcast error $e$ and (ii) a scalar loss/perturbation signal. In particular it never reads a downstream layer's weight-transpose $W_{\ell+1}^\top$ or per-layer error $\delta_{\ell+1}$.
\end{definition}
Reusing a layer's \emph{own} cached forward tensors is permitted (plain DFA already reuses $\phi'(z_\ell)$). The distinction that matters is between reading a downstream \emph{weight} (transport) and reusing a cached \emph{activation} (not transport). We verify Def.~\ref{def:lockfree} operationally with a two-part probe (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\subsection{The Jacobian decomposition}
A block receives its incoming error as a direct broadcast of the output error; we use one broadcast point \emph{per pre-LN sublayer}, $e_{\text{attn}}\!\approx\!\partial\mathcal L/\partial x_1$ and $e_{\text{mlp}}\!\approx\!\partial\mathcal L/\partial x_2$, which is both the lock-free decomposition and what makes the value-path exact (below). Tracing the exact backward through Eqs.~\eqref{eq:qkv}--\eqref{eq:mlp} and tagging each factor:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
gradient & factor & status \\
\midrule
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial W_O$ & $\mathrm{Ctx}^\top e_{\text{attn}}$ & \textbf{exact} (both operands available) \\
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial \mathrm{Ctx}$ & $e_{\text{attn}} W_O^\top$ & transport $\to$ surrogate $R_O$ \\
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial V$ & $A^\top(\cdot)$ & $A$ cached (exact) \\
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial A$ & $(\cdot)V^\top$ & $V$ cached (exact) \\
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial (QK^\top)$ & $J_A(\cdot)$ & softmax-Jac from cached $A$ (exact) \\
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial Q,\partial K$ & $(\cdot)K,\,(\cdot)^\top Q$ & $Q,K$ cached (exact) \\
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial W_{Q,K,V}$ & $y^\top(\cdot)$ & $y$ cached (exact) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The \emph{only} transport step in the attention sublayer is $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathrm{Ctx}=e_{\text{attn}}W_O^\top$; \ARDFA{} replaces $W_O^\top$ with a surrogate $R_O$ and routes everything else through the cached data-maps exactly. The MLP sublayer is identical in form: $\partial\mathcal L/\partial W_2=H^\top e_{\text{mlp}}$ is exact, one surrogate $R_2$ replaces $W_2^\top$, and the nonlinearity mask $\phi'$ is reused exactly. The fixed-DFA baseline, by contrast, broadcasts $e_{\text{attn}}$ to each of $Q,K,V$ through independent random matrices and \emph{ignores $A$ entirely}; the ablation in Sec.~\ref{sec:res-attn} isolates the win to the reuse of $A$.

\begin{remark}[What is exact and what is not]
\ARDFA{} tracks the input-conditioned \emph{mixing} ($A$, $J_A$, LN scale, $\phi'$) exactly; the input-conditioned \emph{projection} through $W_{\{Q,K,V,O,1,2\}}$ is approximated, because it factors as (fixed weight)$\times$(input) and the weight half is forbidden. The benefit is thus asymmetric: strong on the value/$A$-contraction path, weaker on the score path ($W_Q,W_K$), which sits behind the random surrogate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Perturbation-Taught surrogate adaptation}
Left fixed and random, $R_O$ bottlenecks the score path. We adapt it \emph{transport-free} by a Kolen--Pollack-style regression toward $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathrm{Ctx}$, where that target is estimated by antithetic \emph{node perturbation} of $\mathrm{Ctx}$ scored by a forward-only recompute of the (linearized) block loss---no autograd, no $W_O^\top$:
\begin{equation}
\hat g_{\mathrm{Ctx}} = \frac{\mathcal L(\mathrm{Ctx}+\xi)-\mathcal L(\mathrm{Ctx}-\xi)}{2\rho^2}\,\xi,\qquad
R_O \leftarrow R_O + \eta_R\, e_{\text{attn}}^\top\big(\hat g_{\mathrm{Ctx}} - e_{\text{attn}} R_O\big). \label{eq:pt}
\end{equation}
We refer to Eq.~\eqref{eq:pt} as \emph{Perturbation-Taught} feedback (\PT). It reads only the broadcast error, a scalar loss difference, and its own state, so it preserves Def.~\ref{def:lockfree}. Its cost, and its ceiling, are analyzed in Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}: the estimator variance scales with the perturbed dimension, and the update's fixed point is a fixed linear map.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}
\subsection{Value-path exactness}
\begin{proposition}[Value-path exactness]\label{prop:vpe}
Fix a block and suppose the attention sublayer receives the broadcast error $e_{\text{attn}}=\partial\mathcal L/\partial x_1$ and the MLP sublayer $e_{\text{mlp}}=\partial\mathcal L/\partial x_2$. Then the \ARDFA{} gradients for the output projections are exactly the backpropagation gradients, independent of the surrogates $R_O,R_2$:
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{W_O}^{\ARDFA}\mathcal L = \mathrm{Ctx}^\top e_{\text{attn}} = \nabla_{W_O}^{\mathrm{BP}}\mathcal L,\qquad
\nabla_{W_2}^{\ARDFA}\mathcal L = H^\top e_{\text{mlp}} = \nabla_{W_2}^{\mathrm{BP}}\mathcal L .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
$x_1=x+\mathrm{Ctx}\,W_O$ depends on $W_O$ only through the linear term $\mathrm{Ctx}\,W_O$, whose local Jacobian is $\mathrm{Ctx}$; the chain rule gives $\nabla_{W_O}\mathcal L=\mathrm{Ctx}^\top(\partial\mathcal L/\partial x_1)$. Both $\mathrm{Ctx}$ (cached) and $e_{\text{attn}}=\partial\mathcal L/\partial x_1$ (broadcast) are available without transport; the surrogate enters only the \emph{downstream} $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathrm{Ctx}$, not this term. The MLP case is identical. \end{proof}
Empirically, the \ARDFA{} value-path gradients match a causal autograd reference to $\le 3.1\times 10^{-15}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}, GUARD~1), confirming both the derivation and the implementation.

\subsection{Alignment floor and its decay}
\begin{lemma}[Depth-dependent alignment floor]\label{lem:floor}
Let $\rho_\ell$ be the cosine alignment between the transport-free update and the true gradient at layer $\ell$. For fixed random broadcast feedback the expected floor scales as $\Theta(1/\sqrt{L})$ in depth $L$; row-orthonormal (structured) feedback preserves error norm and raises the floor, but does not remove the depth dependence.
\end{lemma}
Lemma~\ref{lem:floor} is the standard structured-feedback intuition made quantitative; crucially, it predicts that alignment \emph{decays} with depth. Our depth study (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-depth}) confirms this as a pre-registered negative control: the worst-layer angle of fixed-DFA grows monotonically with depth (slope $+3.26^{\circ}$/layer, CI excludes $0$). This repairs a gap in the earlier version of the theory, which \emph{assumed} a sign-advantage $p>\tfrac12$ and a positive alignment rather than deriving them; here these quantities are \emph{measured}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}'s sign is validated rather than posited.

\subsection{Why Perturbation-Taught feedback lifts the level but not the slope}
\begin{proposition}[Fixed-linear ceiling of \PT]\label{prop:ceiling}
The expected fixed point of the update~\eqref{eq:pt} is the linear regression map $R_O^\star = \E[\hat g_{\mathrm{Ctx}}\,e^\top]\,\E[e\,e^\top]^{-1}$. Consequently $R_O^\star$ is a \emph{fixed} linear function of the broadcast error and cannot track an input-conditioned target; \PT{} can lower the alignment error \emph{level} but cannot change its scaling with depth. Moreover, the single-sample node-perturbation estimator has variance $\propto d_{\mathrm{pert}}$, so reaching a target averaged cosine requires $N=\Theta(d_{\mathrm{pert}})$ samples.
\end{proposition}
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} predicts exactly the empirical pattern below: \PT{} improves mid-depth alignment (a level shift) but leaves the depth-slope unchanged (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-depth}), and its surrogate only \emph{partially} recovers $W_O^\top$ at realistic width (relative error $1.06$; Sec.~\ref{sec:res-attn}). The gain at scale therefore comes from \ARDFA{}'s exact $A$-reuse, not from \PT{} reaching exactness.

\subsection{Stationarity, read honestly}
For completeness we record the biased-SGD view. With a $\beta$-smooth loss and a transport-free direction $\tilde g_t$, the descent lemma gives
$\tfrac1T\sum_t \E\|\nabla\mathcal L(V_t)\|^2 \le \tfrac{2(\mathcal L_0-\mathcal L_\star)}{\eta T} + C_1\beta\eta\sigma^2 + C_2(1-2p)^2 + C_0\sum_\ell(1-\bar\rho_\ell)$,
a standard bound in the sign-advantage $p$ and per-layer alignment $\bar\rho_\ell$. We stress what this does and does not say: it is a convergence statement \emph{conditional} on $p$ and $\bar\rho_\ell$, which our experiments treat as measured quantities, not free assumptions. It does not bound the \emph{solution quality} relative to BP, and Sec.~\ref{sec:gap} shows empirically that the achievable stationary point is substantially worse.

\section{Experimental Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
All experiments are pre-registered with numeric gates and run in float64 where a reference gradient is needed, on CPU with pinned threads and fixed seeds. Autograd is used \emph{only} to produce exact-BP references and alignment targets; the transport-free strategies compute their own gradients explicitly. We report mean $\pm$ 95\% CIs over $n\ge3$ seeds. Guards enforced at every stage:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{GUARD 1 (value-path exactness).} \ARDFA{}'s $\nabla_{W_O},\nabla_{W_2}$ match a causal autograd reference to $<10^{-5}$ (measured $\le3.1\times10^{-15}$).
  \item \textbf{GUARD 2 (transport-free).} An AST-level check confirms the surrogate-adaptation path contains no autograd call and no $W_O^\top$; a \emph{positive control} (a variant that does read $W_O^\top$) must be flagged, and an $e$-fixed weight-perturbation leaves \ARDFA{}'s gradients invariant (change $=0$) while changing the positive control ($\approx29$--$33$).
  \item \textbf{Alignment metric.} Per-weight-matrix cosine to the exact reference, reported as the worst (max-angle) matrix; $\theta=\arccos(\rho)$ in degrees; depth-slope by OLS of $\theta_{\min}(L)$.
\end{itemize}
Artifacts (code, JSON results, seeds) reproduce every table.

\subsection{Economics: throughput is not GPU-dollars}\label{sec:econ}
DFA's only genuine efficiency axis is backward \emph{parallelism}, which materializes only in pipeline-parallel training. An analytical cost model (forward $t_f{=}1$, backward $t_b{=}2$; \ARDFA-pipeline vs tuned 1F1B$+$checkpointing) gives throughput ratios of $1.15\times$ when the per-stage update cost $t_u\!\approx\!t_b$, $1.66\times$ when $t_u\!\approx\!t_f$, and $2.2$--$2.85\times$ when memory pressure forces BP (but not the $O(1)$-activation \ARDFA) to recompute. So the pipeline win is real but \emph{conditional} on the unmeasured $t_u$, and the model omits the $e$-broadcast communication, which erodes it further.

This throughput number, however, does not save compute, because the relevant quantity is \textbf{wall-clock to a target loss} $=$ (steps-to-target) $\times$ (time-per-step). Sec.~\ref{sec:res-acc} shows \ARDFA{} converges to a loss BP surpasses, so time-to-BP-quality is unbounded regardless of throughput. Finally, a transport-fraction frontier---train with update $(1{-}\lambda)\,\text{DFA} + \lambda\,\text{BP}$---shows accuracy recovers roughly \emph{in proportion} to $\lambda$: $25\%$ of the gap at $\lambda{=}0.10$ and $52\%$ at $\lambda{=}0.25$. There is no cheap-closure regime; buying accuracy costs transport, which is precisely the parallelism one set out to save.

\section{FeedFlip: Feedback-Driven Bit-Flip Training}\label{sec:feedflip}
We now use the feedback signal in the setting that names the project: training ternary networks by flipping bits directly, with no float shadow weights.

\paragraph{The mechanism.} Each weight is stored only as a ternary value $w\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ and a small signed integer accumulator $c$. At each step we form a per-weight descent vote from the \emph{sign} of the gradient estimate and integrate it, flipping the bit when the evidence crosses a threshold $K$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&c \leftarrow c - \mathrm{sign}(\hat g),\\
&\text{if } c \ge K:\ w \leftarrow \min(w{+}1,+1),\ c\leftarrow 0;\quad
\text{if } c \le -K:\ w \leftarrow \max(w{-}1,-1),\ c\leftarrow 0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The forward pass uses $w$ with a fixed per-layer scale. There is no float shadow and no full gradient---only a \emph{sign}. State per weight collapses from a $32$-bit shadow to $\approx\!1.58 + \log_2(2K{+}1)$ bits (e.g.\ $5.7$ bits at $K{=}8$). Because a flip needs only the sign, DFA is the natural feedback: it supplies that sign transport-free, shadow-free, and lock-free.

\paragraph{The mechanism works---with a good sign.} On a deep MLP (teacher task, $n{=}3$; Table~\ref{tab:flip-mlp}), sign-driven flipping with the \emph{exact} (BP) sign matches float-shadow BP ($0.629$ vs $0.619$) at $5.6\times$ less state and $\approx\!2\times$ faster steps. The bit-flip idea is real. Transport-free flipping is partially viable here: vote-averaging (larger $K$) plus orthogonal feedback compound to $0.548$---within $0.08$ of BP, transport-free, at $\approx\!4\times$ less state---but only partially.

\paragraph{Why: cosine alignment is not sign correctness.} The mechanistic explanation is the last column of Table~\ref{tab:flip-tf}. A bit-flip consumes only the per-weight \emph{sign} of the gradient, and the fraction of weights whose transport-free sign matches the true gradient is $p\!\approx\!0.53$ for \emph{both} fixed-DFA and \ARDFA{}---barely above the $0.5$ chance line---\emph{even though} \ARDFA{} improves the aggregate \emph{cosine} alignment from $90^{\circ}$ to $46^{\circ}$ (Table~\ref{tab:attn}). A large cosine gain can come almost entirely from a few high-magnitude coordinates and a global rotation while leaving most per-coordinate signs unchanged; the flip rule, which weights every coordinate equally by sign, is blind to exactly the improvement \ARDFA{} provides. \textbf{Cosine alignment---the standard DFA diagnostic---is the wrong proxy for discrete, transport-free learning; per-weight sign correctness is what a bit-flip needs, and it is the quantity that does not improve.}

\section{The Sign Barrier}\label{sec:gap}
The flip experiments and the continuous-training experiments (Secs.~\ref{sec:res-acc}--\ref{sec:econ}) tell one story from two directions.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{The FeedFlip mechanism is a genuine efficiency win}---shadow-free, sign-driven, $\approx\!5.6\times$ less state, $\approx\!2\times$ faster---\emph{given} an accurate sign.
  \item \textbf{Transport-free feedback supplies direction, not per-weight sign.} \ARDFA{} maximizes the aggregate (cosine) direction achievable transport-free, yet its per-weight sign-match stays near chance. The discrete regime, which uses only signs, therefore gains nothing; and the continuous regime, which can exploit magnitude, still does not reach competitive accuracy, per-step cost, or wall-clock-to-target.
  \item \textbf{The two failures share one cause.} The transport-fraction frontier shows continuous accuracy recovers only in proportion to reintroduced transport, and the flip study shows the sign it needs is not there transport-free. Both are the same wall: closing the gap---in signs or in magnitudes---demands the transport DFA removes, and the methods that supply it (predictive coding, target propagation) re-lock the backward.
\end{itemize}
The practical implication is a redirection with a sharper target. Effort spent improving \emph{cosine} alignment (the usual objective of better-feedback methods) has diminishing returns for either regime; the open problem is a transport-free rule whose \emph{per-weight sign} is reliably correct at depth and through attention. Our evidence suggests this is hard, and---given that the sign barrier and the accuracy wall coincide---possibly the same problem.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
Our study is deliberately laptop-scale and CPU-only: single-head blocks, small corpora, and modest depth, chosen so that every claim is exactly reproducible and the reference gradients are affordable. The FeedFlip experiments share this scope (deep MLP and a small ternary Transformer, $n{=}3$), and the sign-match $p$ is measured over nonzero-true-gradient entries at a few training snapshots; the efficiency claim for the mechanism is established with an \emph{accurate} sign at this scale, not at LM scale. The pipeline cost model is analytical and not yet validated against a multi-process simulator or GPU hardware; in particular $t_u$ (the per-stage update cost) is the pivotal unmeasured quantity, and the $e$-broadcast communication is omitted. The transport-fraction frontier is measured on a task with a modest DFA--BP gap and is suggestive rather than decisive at the large-gap LM scale. These bound the strength of the positive claim (a real but small-scale mechanism) more than the negative one, which is corroborated across independent axes (discrete flips and continuous training). Finally, the convolutional DFA baseline in Table~\ref{tab:cnn-gap} is sensitive to the learning rate and exhibits epoch-to-epoch test-accuracy oscillation even at its stable setting; Appendix~\ref{app:dfa-lr} characterizes this envelope.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
FeedFlipNets trains ternary networks by feedback-driven bit-flips: no float shadow weights, a per-weight sign vote flips a bit when evidence accrues, and---because a flip needs only the sign---DFA supplies that signal transport-free and shadow-free. The mechanism is genuinely efficient given an accurate sign ($\approx\!5.6\times$ less state, $\approx\!2\times$ faster than float-shadow BP). To provide a better transport-free sign we introduced Activation-Routed DFA, which reuses a Transformer block's cached data-maps and surrogates only the weight transposes, yielding exact value-path gradients and cutting the worst-case attention \emph{cosine}-alignment angle from $90^{\circ}$ to $46^{\circ}$. The central finding is negative and precise: this alignment gain does not help. On a ternary Transformer, transport-free flipping is worse than not flipping, and \ARDFA{} does not beat vanilla DFA, because per-weight \emph{sign}-match stays near chance even as cosine alignment improves sharply---and the same barrier shows up on the continuous side as an unclosed accuracy and wall-clock-to-target gap. The lasting message: cosine alignment is the wrong objective for transport-free learning; per-weight sign correctness is the binding constraint, in the discrete regime directly and in the continuous regime through the transport-fraction frontier. We hope the efficient bit-flip mechanism, and especially the honestly-negative sign-barrier characterization, help others target the right quantity.

\section{Convolutional DFA learning-rate sensitivity}\label{app:dfa-lr}
Table~\ref{tab:dfa-lr-sweep} reports a learning-rate sweep for the convolutional DFA baseline of Table~\ref{tab:cnn-gap} (Fashion-MNIST, single seed, GPU).
