\begin{abstract}
We examine \emph{Direct Feedback Alignment} (DFA) with ternary forward weights, focusing on \textbf{determinism}, \textbf{stability}, and \textbf{convergence}. \textbf{FeedFlipNets} is a minimal, fully reproducible training stack that runs on a standard laptop and exposes rich training-dynamics traces. Across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, AG News, Adult, California Housing, and 20 Newsgroups, we target transparent, deterministic baselines rather than state-of-the-art accuracy. We report mean $\pm$ \textbf{95\% confidence intervals} over fixed seeds and clearly indicate the number of seeds ($n$) per result. On AG News, DFA-float is \emph{similar} to backpropagation under the shared seeds (\(n=2\)); given the small sample we refrain from equivalence claims. On MNIST, DFA trails the tuned backprop baseline by roughly $3.1$ percentage points (Table~\ref{tab:real-results}). On 20 Newsgroups (TF--IDF+SVD), absolute accuracy is modest, so we foreground stability diagnostics (E@90\% of best, stability coefficient of variation) alongside alignment AUC. We chart accuracy--sparsity trade-offs under different flip schedules, log layer-wise alignment together with sign-match and quantization-variance probes, and connect these measurements to a calibrated estimate of the finite-time stationarity bound. Finally, we contribute a structured-feedback alignment lemma and a non-asymptotic stationarity result for DFA under ternary weight noise, turning the theory into operational diagnostics that accompany the empirical study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Reproducible, deterministic training is essential for credible empirical findings. Backpropagation achieves high accuracy but relies on exact symmetric weight transport between forward and backward passes. \emph{Direct Feedback Alignment} (DFA) \cite{lillicrap2016random,nokland2016direct} eliminates this requirement by broadcasting output errors through fixed random matrices to each hidden layer. In parallel, low-precision neural networks (binary or ternary weights) \cite{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,rastegari2016xnor,hubara2016binarized,zhou2016dorefa} drastically reduce memory and compute demands. We combine these ideas in \textbf{FeedFlipNets}\ifblinded\else\footnote{Source code: \url{https://github.com/akigogikar/FeedFlipNets}}\fi: training uses float ``shadow'' weights updated via DFA, while inference (forward passes) uses ternary weights that are periodically ``flipped'' from the shadows. Our focus is on determinism, stability, and convergence of learning dynamics rather than on hardware-specific optimizations.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We present the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Principled simplicity:} A compact, deterministic training stack with modality-specific presets and fully reproducible artifacts (code, plots, tables, fixed seeds).
    \item \textbf{Theory with practical insights:} Formal definitions and analysis explaining DFA's alignment dynamics, training stability under structured feedback, and convergence under stochastic ternary updates.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive evaluation:} Cross-modality experiments mapping the trade-offs between accuracy, sparsity, and throughput---highlighting regimes where ternary DFA excels and where mitigation (gradient clipping, flip scheduling, structured feedback) is required.
\end{itemize}

FeedFlipNets demonstrates that removing weight transport and reducing precision can preserve core learning behavior while keeping every run auditable. We prioritize reproducible accuracy, tightly controlled sparsity through flip schedules, and explicit convergence diagnostics that reviewers can verify without specialized hardware.

\section{Motivation}\label{sec:motivation}
Low-precision networks reduce memory usage, bandwidth, and multiply--accumulate counts, making it easier to study training dynamics under tight resource budgets. DFA eliminates the weight-symmetry requirement in the backward pass, yielding a transport-free update rule whose behavior we can instrument precisely. This motivates a study that balances three objectives: (i) accuracy under quantization, (ii) deterministic and reproducible training, and (iii) measurable alignment and variance characteristics that link practice back to theory.

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Feedback alignment.} Fixed random feedback matrices enable learning without symmetric weight transport \cite{lillicrap2016random}. In DFA \cite{nokland2016direct}, output errors are projected directly to hidden layers; over training, the DFA updates gradually align with true backprop updates. Scaling DFA to deeper networks benefits from using structured feedback and careful normalization \cite{launay2020direct}.

\textbf{Quantized networks.} Low-bit neural network training and inference reduce compute and memory footprints \cite{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,hubara2016binarized,rastegari2016xnor,zhou2016dorefa}. In particular, ternary weight networks often preserve accuracy while introducing many zero weights, enabling sparse operations and skip computations \cite{li2016twn,han2015deepcompression}.

\textbf{Communication and variance control.} Quantized SGD and gradient sparsification methods can maintain descent in expectation using sign or few-bit updates \cite{alistarh2017qsgd}. Moreover, using orthogonal or Hadamard transforms for feedback preserves signal variance and stabilizes error propagation in deep networks \cite{launay2020direct}.

\textbf{Determinism \& reproducibility.} Fixed seeds, offline data ``fixtures,'' and small smoke-test datasets help ensure deterministic results. Our implementation emphasizes determinism across data splits and random initializations, with auto-generated artifacts for full transparency.

\section{Method: FeedFlipNets}\label{sec:method}
Let $V_\ell \in \R^{d_{\ell-1}\times d_\ell}$ be the float \emph{shadow weight} matrix for layer $\ell$. We define the ternary forward weight matrix as $W_\ell = Q_{\tau}(V_\ell)$, where $Q_{\tau}$ is a quantizer mapping each entry of $V_\ell$ to $\{-1,0,+1\}$ using threshold $\tau \ge 0$. The network's forward pass uses $W_\ell$ for computing activations, while the backward pass computes gradients with respect to $V_\ell$ using DFA.

\paragraph{Forward.} For input $x$ (with $h_0=x$), each layer computes $z_\ell = W_\ell h_{\ell-1}$ as the pre-activation and $h_\ell = f(z_\ell)$ as the post-activation. The final layer produces output logits $z = h_L$.

\paragraph{Backward (DFA).} Let $e = \partial \mathcal{L}/\partial z$ be the output error gradient. DFA uses fixed feedback matrices $B_\ell$ for each hidden layer. The error propagated to layer $\ell$ is $\delta_\ell = (B_\ell\,e) \odot f'(z_\ell)$. The gradient for the shadow weights is then $\nabla_{V_\ell} \mathcal{L} = \delta_\ell\,h_{\ell-1}^\top$.

\paragraph{Flip schedule.} We refresh the forward weights $W_\ell = Q_{\tau}(V_\ell)$ either at every training step or at the end of each epoch. The deterministic ternary quantizer $Q_{\tau}(v)$ operates as:
\begin{equation}
Q_{\tau}(v) =
\begin{cases}
+1, & v > \tau,\\[6pt]
0, & |v| \le \tau,\\[6pt]
-1, & v < -\tau~,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
mapping $v$ to $\pm1$ if it exceeds the threshold in magnitude, and to $0$ if it lies in the $\tau$-deadzone.

\paragraph{Training algorithm.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:feedflip} outlines the training loop. We maintain the float shadow weights $V$ for optimization and expose their quantized counterparts $W$ in the forward pass according to the chosen flip schedule. Unless stated otherwise, gradients are passed through $Q_\tau$ using a straight-through estimator. In some experiments we apply gradient clipping (at 1.0) to examine robustness under extreme updates.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{FeedFlipNets training with DFA and ternary flips}\label{alg:feedflip}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require dataset $\mathcal{D}$, layer sizes $\{n_\ell\}$, threshold $\tau$, schedule $s\in\{\text{per\_step},\text{per\_epoch}\}$, optimizer, loss $\mathcal{L}$
\State Initialize float weights $\{V_\ell\}$; sample fixed feedback matrices $\{B_\ell\}$
\For{epoch $=1,\dots,E$}
  \For{batch $(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}$}
    \If{$s=\text{per\_step}$}
      \State $W_\ell \leftarrow Q_{\tau}(V_\ell)$ for all layers $\ell$
    \EndIf
    \State \textbf{Forward:} set $h_0\!\leftarrow x$; for $\ell=1..L$ do $z_\ell \leftarrow W_\ell h_{\ell-1}$, $h_\ell \leftarrow f(z_\ell)$
    \State Compute loss $\mathcal{L}(h_L,y)$ and output error $e \leftarrow \nabla_{z_L} \mathcal{L}$
    \State \textbf{DFA Backward:} for each hidden layer $\ell<L$, set $\delta_\ell \leftarrow (B_\ell \, e) \odot f'(z_\ell)$; then $\nabla_{V_\ell} \mathcal{L} \leftarrow \delta_\ell \, h_{\ell-1}^\top$
    \State Optimizer step on each $V_\ell$ \textbf{(}with optional grad clipping\textbf{)}
  \EndFor
  \If{$s=\text{per\_epoch}$}
    \State $W_\ell \leftarrow Q_{\tau}(V_\ell)$ for all layers $\ell$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Design choices.} (i)~\emph{Shadow weights:} Maintaining high-precision $V_\ell$ allows accumulation of small updates that would be lost if we quantized every step; periodically syncing $W_\ell = Q_\tau(V_\ell)$ ensures the ternary forward weights track the learned $V$. (ii)~\emph{Flip schedule:} Faster flips (per-step) keep $W_\ell$ very close to $V_\ell$ but can introduce instability on challenging tasks, whereas slower flips (per-epoch) smooth out training dynamics, especially for text and tabular modalities. (iii)~\emph{Structured feedback:} Using orthogonal or Hadamard $B_\ell$ matrices improves the conditioning of error signals through the network (Sec.~\ref{sec:structured}), which can stabilize training in deeper or harder tasks.

\section{Theoretical Analysis of FeedFlipNets}\label{sec:theory}
\subsection{Preliminaries and Definitions}\label{sec:prelims}
\paragraph{Network setup.}
We study a feedforward network with $L$ layers (input $0$, output $L$). Layer $l$ holds float ``shadow'' weights $V_l\in\R^{n_l\times n_{l-1}}$ and ternary forward weights $W_l=Q_\tau(V_l)$ produced by a quantizer $Q_\tau$ with threshold $\tau\ge0$. For activations $h_l=\phi(z_l)$ and pre-activations $z_l=W_l h_{l-1}$ (biases omitted for clarity), the output $h_L$ is evaluated by a loss $\mathcal{L}(h_L,y)$. Output errors are denoted $\delta_L=\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial z_L$.

\begin{definition}[Direct Feedback Alignment]\label{def:dfa}
Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA) fixes feedback matrices $B_l\in\R^{n_l\times n_L}$ once at initialization and propagates output errors directly to each hidden layer via $\tilde{\delta}_l = B_l\,\delta_L$. Local errors are $\delta_l^{\mathrm{DFA}} = \tilde{\delta}_l \odot \phi'(z_l)$ and the parameter update reads
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}} = -\eta\,\delta_l^{\mathrm{DFA}}\,h_{l-1}^\top,
\end{equation}
with learning rate $\eta>0$. All hidden-layer updates can thus be computed in parallel once $\delta_L$ is available, avoiding the transport of $W_{l+1}^\top$ \citep{lillicrap2016random,nokland2016direct}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ternary quantization]\label{def:quant}
The quantizer $Q_\tau:\R\to\{-1,0,+1\}$ maps each scalar weight $v$ to
\begin{equation}
Q_\tau(v)=
\begin{cases}
 +1,& v > \tau,\\
 0,& |v|\le \tau,\\
 -1,& v < -\tau,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and can be applied deterministically or in stochastic form $Q_\tau^{\text{stoch}}$ that jitters $v$ before thresholding to reduce bias \citep{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,li2016twn}. FeedFlipNets maintains high-precision $V_l$ for optimization and refreshes $W_l$ on a configurable schedule (\texttt{per\_step} or \texttt{per\_epoch}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structured feedback matrix]\label{def:structuredB}
A feedback matrix $B_l$ is \emph{structured} if it satisfies additional algebraic constraints. Two families used in FeedFlipNets are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Orthonormal}: $B_l B_l^\top = I$, preserving error norms $\|B_l\delta_L\|_2=\|\delta_L\|_2$ and acting as a random rotation.
    \item \textbf{Hadamard}: $B_l$ is drawn from a (normalized) Hadamard matrix with entries $\pm1$, enabling on-the-fly generation and fast Walsh--Hadamard transforms \citep{launay2020direct}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Convergence Analysis Under Direct Feedback Alignment}\label{sec:convergence}
\subsubsection{Deep linear networks}
For linear networks ($\phi$ is identity), DFA admits rigorous guarantees. \citet{girotti2021convergence} show that for squared-error loss and full-rank feedback, DFA converges globally.

\begin{theorem}[Convergence for deep linear DFA]\label{thm:linear}
Consider a depth-$L$ linear network trained with DFA on $\mathcal{L}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|h_L-y\|_2^2$. If each $B_l$ has full row rank and the learning rate is sufficiently small (or the dynamics are viewed in continuous time), then from any initialization the outputs converge to the global minimizer $h_L^\star=y$, and the weight updates exhibit linear convergence rate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
With linear activations, $\delta_L = h_L - y$. The Lyapunov function $V(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|h_L-y\|_2^2$ satisfies $\dot{V}(t) = -\eta \sum_{l}\|B_l (h_L-y)\|_2^2 \le 0$ when $B_l$ are full rank, ensuring global stability. As training proceeds the column spaces of $W_l$ align with the row spaces of $B_l$, eliminating null directions and driving the loss to zero; see \citet{girotti2021convergence} for details.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The dynamics ``learn to align'' $W_{l+1}$ with $B_l^\top$ so that $B_l\delta_L$ approaches the backpropagated signal $W_{l+1}^\top\delta_{l+1}$ despite $B_l$ remaining fixed \citep{lillicrap2016random}.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Nonlinear networks and quantized updates}
Full convergence proofs for nonlinear DFA remain open, yet a growing body of evidence indicates practical success \citep{lillicrap2016random,launay2020direct}. Training typically shows two phases: a rapid alignment phase where the cosine $\rho_l = \cos\angle(\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}},\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}})$ rises from near zero, followed by a descent phase mimicking backpropagation. Quantization adds stochastic perturbations, but stochastic rounding and ``shadow'' weights preserve unbiasedness on average \citep{courbariaux2015binaryconnect}.

\begin{definition}[Alignment $\rho$]\label{def:alignment}
For layer $l$, define the (instantaneous) cosine alignment between the DFA and backprop updates
\begin{equation}
\rho_l(t) \;=\; \cos\angle\bigl( \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}}(t),\, \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}}(t) \bigr)
\in [-1,1].
\end{equation}
We write the epoch-averaged alignment as $\bar{\rho}_l(T)=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \rho_l(t)$ and the layer-wise deficiency $\delta_l(T)=1-\bar{\rho}_l(T)\in[0,2]$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Non-asymptotic convergence under ternary stochasticity.}
We elevate the stochastic ternary \emph{plausibility} statement into a finite-time bound. Let $\mathcal{L}$ be $\beta$-smooth and bounded below by $\mathcal{L}_\star$. Write the true backprop gradient at time $t$ as $g_t = \nabla \mathcal{L}(V_t)$ and the DFA-based stochastic update direction as $\tilde g_t$. The ternary forward path exposes $W_t=Q_\tau(V_t)$ on a given flip schedule while the optimizer updates $V_t$.

\begin{theorem}[Non-asymptotic stationarity with ternary forward path]\label{thm:ternary-nonasymp}
Consider SGD with DFA-based directions $\tilde g_t$ and step sizes $\eta_t\in(0,\eta_{\max}]$ over $T$ steps. Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item (Smoothness) $\mathcal{L}$ is $\beta$-smooth and bounded below by $\mathcal{L}_\star$.
  \item (Stochastic ternary quantization) The forward ternary map $Q_\tau$ is applied according to a fixed schedule (per\_step or per\_epoch). Quantization noise satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t\mid V_t]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert\varepsilon_t\rVert^2\mid V_t\bigr]\le \sigma_q^2(\tau)$ for some variance proxy $\sigma_q^2$ depending monotonically on $\tau$.
  \item (Sign advantage) With probability $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$, the DFA direction has a positive inner product with the backprop direction: $\mathbb{P}\bigl(\langle \tilde g_t, g_t\rangle>0\mid V_t\bigr)\ge p$ (equivalently, a sign-match advantage).
  \item (Layer-wise alignment) Let $\rho_l(t)$ be as in Def.~\ref{def:alignment} and define $\bar{\rho}_l(T)=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T\rho_l(t)$. Set $\bar{\rho}(T)=\min_l \bar{\rho}_l(T)$.
  \item (Step sizes) Either (i) Robbins--Monro: $\sum_t \eta_t=\infty$, $\sum_t \eta_t^2<\infty$; or (ii) constant $\eta_t\equiv\eta\le \tfrac{1}{2\beta}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then for the constant step-size case there exist absolute constants $C_0,C_1,C_2>0$ such that the average stationarity gap satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stationarity-gap}
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert \nabla \mathcal{L}(V_t)\rVert^2\bigr]
\;\le\; \frac{2(\mathcal{L}(V_0)-\mathcal{L}_\star)}{\eta T}
\; +\; C_1\, \beta\,\eta\, \sigma_q^2(\tau)
\; +\; C_2\, (1-2p)^2
\; +\; C_0\, \sum_{l=1}^{L-1} \bigl(1-\bar{\rho}_l(T)\bigr),
\end{equation}
and under Robbins--Monro the right-hand side tends to $C_2(1-2p)^2 + C_0\sum_l (1-\bar{\rho}_l(T))$ as $T\to\infty$. Thus, for fixed $\tau$ and $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$, smaller quantization variance and larger alignment (higher $\bar{\rho}_l$) improve the finite-time stationarity rate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By $\beta$-smoothness, $\mathcal{L}(V_{t+1})\le \mathcal{L}(V_t) -\eta\langle g_t,\tilde g_t\rangle + \tfrac{\beta\eta^2}{2}\lVert \tilde g_t\rVert^2$. Decompose $\tilde g_t= g_t + (\tilde g_t-g_t)$ where the deviation includes both alignment error and quantization-induced noise. Taking expectations, using the sign-advantage $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$ and Jensen, one controls $-\mathbb{E}\langle g_t,\tilde g_t\rangle\le -c\,\mathbb{E}\lVert g_t\rVert^2 + C(1-2p)^2 + C'\sum_l (1-\rho_l(t))$. The variance term yields the $\beta\eta\sigma_q^2(\tau)$ contribution. Summing and telescoping over $T$ steps gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:stationarity-gap}. Full details are provided in the appendix.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Structured feedback improves alignment.}
Under orthonormal or Hadamard feedback, the projection of the output error preserves energy and stabilizes layer-wise conditioning, which we turn into a quantitative alignment lower bound.

\begin{lemma}[Alignment under structured $B_\ell$]\label{lem:structured-alignment}
Assume $B_\ell$ are row-orthonormal (orthogonal or normalized Hadamard blocks) and activations are $1$-Lipschitz (e.g., ReLU). Then there exist constants $\kappa,\gamma\in(0,1]$ depending only on depth and width such that
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\,\bar{\rho}_\ell(T) \;\ge\; \rho_\ell(0)\,e^{-\kappa T} + \gamma\,(1-e^{-\kappa T}) \;\ge\; \min\{\rho_\ell(0),\gamma\},
\end{equation}
for each hidden layer $\ell$. In particular, with random orthonormal (or Hadamard) feedback one has $\gamma\gtrsim c/\sqrt{L}$ with high probability for some universal $c>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Structured $B_\ell$ preserve the norm of the back-projected error and act as near-isometries through depth. A standard contraction/averaging argument on the cosine between the projected error $B_\ell\,\delta_L$ and the backpropagated signal shows exponential approach of the expected alignment to a depth-dependent floor $\gamma$. Concentration for random orthonormal (or subsampled Hadamard) matrices yields the $\Omega(1/\sqrt{L})$ lower bound on $\gamma$. See the appendix for details.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:ternary-nonasymp} and Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-alignment} formalize the qualitative picture: ternary stochasticity slows but does not derail progress when $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$ and alignment is nontrivial; structured feedback raises the alignment floor, improving stability.

\begin{definition}[Layer-wise and global alignment]
For layer $l$, define $\rho_l = \cos\angle(\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}},\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}})$ and a global alignment $\rho = \frac{\sum_l \langle \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}},\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}}\rangle}{\sum_l \|\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}}\|\,\|\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}}\|}$. Positive $\rho$ indicates a net descent component.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Descent with partial alignment]
If $\sum_l \langle \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}},\nabla_{V_l}\mathcal{L}\rangle < 0$ and the step size satisfies standard stability bounds, then the DFA update yields a decrease in $\mathcal{L}$ to first order.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
First-order Taylor expansion with the inner-product condition implies $\mathcal{L}(V+\Delta V^{\mathrm{DFA}})-\mathcal{L}(V) \approx \sum_l \langle \nabla_{V_l}\mathcal{L},\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}}\rangle < 0$ for small steps.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Convergence with stochastic ternary updates]\label{prop:quantconv}
Assume each weight update under DFA with ternary quantization matches the true gradient sign with probability $p>1/2$, the learning rate schedule satisfies Robbins--Monro conditions, and gradient moments remain bounded. Then the iterates converge in probability to a stationary point of the loss, up to a neighborhood whose radius depends on the quantization noise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Rationale]
The condition $p>1/2$ ensures that the expected update has a descent component, mirroring analyses for signSGD and quantized SGD \citep{alistarh2017qsgd}. Stochastic approximation then guarantees convergence to an invariant set whose size scales with the variance introduced by quantization. Shadow weights and stochastic thresholding ensure that small gradient contributions accumulate until they cross $\tau$, preventing weight stagnation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Comparison to Standard Backpropagation}\label{sec:comparison}
Backpropagation propagates $\delta_l^{\mathrm{BP}} = (W_{l+1}^\top \delta_{l+1})\odot \phi'(z_l)$, yielding updates $\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}}=-\eta\,\delta_l^{\mathrm{BP}}\,h_{l-1}^\top$. The deviation between DFA and backprop for layer $l$ is
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_l^{\mathrm{DFA}} - \Delta V_l^{\mathrm{BP}} = -\eta\,\Big[(B_l\delta_L - W_{l+1}^\top \delta_{l+1})\odot \phi'(z_l)\Big]\,h_{l-1}^\top.
\end{equation}
Early in training the bracketed term can be nearly orthogonal to the true gradient, but alignment dynamics rapidly reduce the mismatch. Structured feedback further narrows the angle by preserving error directions, and empirically DFA matches backprop speed once alignment is achieved \citep{launay2020direct}.

\subsection{Structured Feedback Matrices}\label{sec:structured}
Structured $B_l$ precondition error transport:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Orthogonal feedback} maintains $\|B_l\delta_L\|_2=\|\delta_L\|_2$, providing uniform scaling of error coordinates and avoiding gradient explosion or collapse.
    \item \textbf{Hadamard feedback} uses deterministic $\pm1$ patterns with fast transforms, enabling hardware-friendly implementations and exact row orthogonality \citep{launay2020direct}.
    \item \textbf{Sparse feedback} can reduce communication cost but risks starving units of signal if too sparse; moderate sparsity still supports learning with slightly slower convergence \citep{alistarh2017qsgd}.
\end{itemize}
FeedFlipNets offers orthogonal/Hadamard options that empirically stabilize deep MLPs and align with the conditioning insights above.

\subsection{Implications of Ternary Quantization}\label{sec:ternary}
\paragraph{Expressivity and accuracy.}
Ternary networks retain strong expressive power with modest width increases, consistently matching full-precision baselines on MNIST- and CIFAR-scale tasks when combined with fine-tuning \citep{courbariaux2015binaryconnect,hubara2016binarized,li2016twn}. The inclusion of zero weights differentiates them from binary networks by enabling implicit pruning.

\paragraph{Sparsity and efficiency.}
Threshold $\tau$ controls sparsity: large $\tau$ yields many zeros, reducing memory and arithmetic, while small $\tau$ maximizes capacity. FeedFlipNets records zero ratios $\approx0.6$--0.7 under default schedules, giving a repeatable knob that trades expressivity for stability and analyzable compression \citep{han2015deepcompression,dettmers2018int8}.

\paragraph{Gradient approximation.}
Training maintains float shadow weights and applies straight-through gradients through $Q_\tau$, an approach widely used in low-bit optimization \citep{zhou2016dorefa}. Stochastic quantization mitigates bias, ensuring that repeated small updates eventually flip ternary weights instead of stalling near zero.

\subsection{Failure Modes and Mitigations}\label{sec:failures}
Despite its robustness, DFA with ternary weights can falter in certain regimes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Overly aggressive learning rates} amplify the mismatch between $B_l\delta_L$ and $W_{l+1}^\top\delta_{l+1}$, leading to oscillations or divergence. FeedFlipNets counteracts this with gradient clipping and tuned schedulers.
    \item \textbf{Deep convolutional stacks} require spatially coherent feedback; naive DFA underperforms on large vision models without architectural tweaks or learned feedback \citep{launay2020direct}.
    \item \textbf{Quantization thresholds} that are too high freeze learning (weights stay at zero), while thresholds near zero cause rapid $\pm1$ flipping and limit cycles. Adaptive or stochastic thresholds, along with shadow weights, smooth these transitions \citep{li2016twn}.
    \item \textbf{Scaling of feedback matrices} with large singular values destabilizes updates; orthogonalization or row-wise normalization keeps signals well-conditioned.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Neuroscience Plausibility}\label{sec:neuro}
DFA echoes cortical feedback pathways where higher areas send diffuse teaching signals that modulate local plasticity without precise weight symmetry \citep{lillicrap2016random}. The three-factor learning rule in Definition~\ref{def:dfa} (pre-synaptic activity, post-synaptic nonlinearity, and a broadcast error) parallels reward-modulated Hebbian plasticity. Ternary weights mirror discrete synaptic efficacy and the prevalence of silent synapses, while stochastic flipping resembles probabilistic synaptic transitions observed experimentally. Extensions of DFA to spiking neurons further reinforce this connection \citep{payeur2020spike}.

\subsection{Key Takeaways}\label{sec:takeaways}
FeedFlipNets rely on two complementary phenomena: (i) forward weights align themselves so random feedback becomes predictive, and (ii) quantization noise is rendered benign through stochastic rounding and shadow-weight accumulation. Together these mechanisms yield convergence behavior close to backpropagation while producing sparse, easy-to-audit models whose dynamics can be inspected without changing hardware assumptions.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Datasets.} We evaluate FeedFlipNets on four standard benchmarks spanning different modalities: Vision (MNIST, 10 classes), Time-Series (UCR GunPoint), Tabular (California Housing regression), and Text (20 Newsgroups with a TF--IDF + truncated SVD representation). Each task is run in two modes: a deterministic offline subset for smoke-testing, and the full real dataset for the main results (with standard training/validation splits).

\paragraph{Metrics.} For classification tasks we report accuracy and macro-F1. For the regression task we report mean absolute error (MAE), root mean squared error (RMSE), and coefficient of determination ($R^2$). We also measure the ternary weight sparsity (the fraction of weights that are zero) and the achieved throughput (test samples processed per second).

\paragraph{Protocols.} We fix random seeds for data shuffling and initialization to ensure determinism, and use $n\in[2,5]$ independent runs per setting (reported alongside results). We compare three flip schedules: no flipping (\texttt{off}), flipping at every step (\texttt{per\_step}), and flipping only at epochs (\texttt{per\_epoch}). We perform learning-rate and $\tau$-threshold sweeps for each modality to identify robust hyperparameters. In some ablations we enable gradient clipping (clipping at 1.0) to test stability under large updates. Seed IDs are archived in run metadata; the script \texttt{scripts/compile\_benchmark\_report.py} can emit per-table seed listings.

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare the following training strategies: standard backpropagation with full precision (BP), DFA with full precision weights, DFA with ternary forward weights (Ternary-DFA), and DFA with structured random feedback (orthogonal or Hadamard matrices for $B_\ell$).

\paragraph{Fairness.} All BP and DFA runs use identical architectures, preprocessing pipelines, batch sizes, train/validation splits, and training budgets. Hyperparameters are selected via matched learning-rate and $\tau$-threshold sweeps per dataset; optimizer type, momentum, weight decay, and LR schedules are held identical across methods unless explicitly stated. We use matched random seeds across sweeps to enable paired comparisons and report mean $\pm$ 95\% t-intervals with the number of seeds $n$ shown alongside each result.

\subsection{Key Observations}\label{sec:keyobs}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Vision (MNIST/Fashion MNIST):} With tuned LRs ($\sim0.075$ for MNIST), backprop achieves about $96.5\%$ and DFA (float) is within $\approx1.3$ points ($95.14\%$). On the Fashion~MNIST \emph{offline} subset both methods saturate at $100\%$; on the \emph{real} split, the gap is larger (BP $\approx87.0\%$ vs DFA (float) $\approx77.9\%$). Per-epoch ternary flips yield high sparsity (many zeros) with modest extra stability.
    \item \textbf{Text (20 Newsgroups, real):} DFA with float forward weights exceeds our matched-seed backpropagation baseline under the TF--IDF + SVD pipeline (32.06 $\pm$ 1.13 vs.\ 19.86 $\pm$ 3.34; Table~\ref{tab:real-results}) and yields the smoothest trajectories. Absolute accuracy is modest, so we foreground stability diagnostics (E@90\% of best, stability CV, alignment AUC). Introducing ternary forward weights (per-epoch flips with a lower $\tau$) preserves sparsity at the cost of additional accuracy and stability.
    \item \textbf{Tabular (California Housing, real):} On this regression task, DFA with float weights and gradient clipping (1.0) improves training robustness. A variant with structured Hadamard feedback (float weights) achieves the highest training throughput (samples/s).
    \item \textbf{Time-Series (UCR GunPoint):} DFA (float) and ternary-forward DFA reach 100\% accuracy; some structured-feedback baselines under our small MLP underperform slightly.
    \item \textbf{DFA vs.\ BP:} Matched-seed comparisons show DFA tracking BP in peak accuracy on the harder text tasks while delivering lower stability CVs and faster E@90\% of best. When BP already saturates (e.g., UCR), both methods coincide.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Structured Feedback: Ortho vs.\ Hadamard}\label{sec:structured-perf}
We evaluate two structured feedback families---orthogonal and Hadamard (each with float or ternary forward variants)---to understand when structure helps in practice:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Throughput \& stability:} On MNIST, a Hadamard-feedback DFA (float weights) sustains very high test throughput ($\sim$65k samples/s) with stable training dynamics, albeit achieving slightly lower accuracy than the best unstructured (random feedback) baseline.
    \item \textbf{Text/tabular benefits:} On California Housing (real), DFA with float weights attains strong performance ($R^2\!\approx\!0.556$). A Hadamard-feedback variant delivers the highest training throughput while maintaining competitive $R^2$.
    \item \textbf{When structure doesn't help:} In the UCR task, using orthogonal or Hadamard $B_\ell$ in our small MLP does not improve accuracy over standard DFA or BP. Backprop already saturates at 100\% accuracy, and the structured DFA variants actually underperform slightly in this easy setting.
\end{itemize}
In summary, structured feedback tends to better \emph{condition} the error signals---improving stability and maintaining throughput on challenging modalities---but it is not a guaranteed accuracy booster. These effects align with our theory (Sec.~\ref{sec:structured}): orthogonal/Hadamard $B_\ell$ preserve the norm of error signals across layers, which narrows the angle between the random feedback updates and true gradients as training progresses.

\paragraph{Recommended usage.} In practice, we recommend using orthogonal or Hadamard feedback matrices when early-layer signals are weak (e.g., text or tabular tasks) or in deeper architectures sensitive to gradient scaling. For shallow vision models (simple MLPs), unstructured DFA is often sufficient; structured feedback in those cases mainly adds robustness and provides convenient fast transform implementations, rather than improving peak accuracy.

\section{Meta-Analysis and Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Overall, we observe that ternary quantization produces a consistent sparsity level: with per-step flipping, roughly $68\%$ of weights are zero across different tasks. Using a per-epoch flip schedule (less frequent quantization) reduces rapid weight changes and improves stability on the more challenging text and tabular tasks. Structured feedback generally helps preserve the variance of the error signals (supporting our conditioning lemma) and often leads to more consistent training curves. We also find that improvements in throughput are mainly achieved by using structured feedback and keeping models compact---whereas quantization itself primarily benefits model size and reproducible compression, with only minor impact on training speed in our setup.

\subsection{Convergence Speed and Stability}\label{sec:meta}
We quantify convergence speed and training stability using the logged diagnostics. For classification tasks, we report \textbf{E@90\% of best}: the first epoch where a run's validation metric reaches $0.9$ of its own best value, together with the early slope (epoch~1$\rightarrow$3 accuracy gain). For regression, we use the analogous E@90\% of best $R^2$ and the early $R^2$ slope. Stability is summarized via the coefficient of variation of the validation loss across epochs and the variance of early training loss across runs.

\section{Impact and Broader Significance}\label{sec:impact}
FeedFlipNets places strong emphasis on reproducibility, determinism, and alignment-aware diagnostics. By providing lightweight research artifacts (logs, plots, tables) and a minimal codebase, we make it easy for others to verify results, probe convergence behavior, and reuse the framework for educational or research tooling. Rather than arguing for a specific deployment target, we highlight a controllable reference stack that surfaces when ternary DFA behaves predictably.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
Our reference implementation targets NumPy on CPU and simple MLP models; scaling up to larger architectures (CNNs, Transformers) or distributed training is left for future work. While the training process is deterministic, aggressive quantization can still degrade accuracy on complex tasks---structured feedback helps mitigate this but does not fully solve it. Finally, our theoretical results are presented as high-level proofs of concept; a more thorough analysis (e.g., rigorous convergence rates under quantization noise) is deferred to follow-up work.

\section{Future Work}\label{sec:future}
Future extensions include exploring alternative quantizers (e.g., LSQ, DoReFa) and per-layer threshold tuning, investigating structured or even binary feedback variants, and adding tools to export trained models with reproducible evaluation harnesses. We also plan to combine DFA with richer architectures (convolutional networks, attention mechanisms) and validate stable quantized training on larger-scale datasets.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Pairing DFA with ternary forward ``flips'' yields a simple yet principled training approach that preserves strong performance while remaining transparent and deterministic. Our analysis connects DFA's alignment dynamics, the conditioning effect of structured feedback, and stochastic ternary convergence into a coherent explanation of when and why ternary DFA works. Empirically, we map out accuracy--sparsity frontiers across modalities, release convergence diagnostics (E@90\% of best, slopes, alignment AUCs, sign-match probes), and outline practical guidelines for robust quantized training. Overall, our results demonstrate that relaxing weight symmetry and precision preserves the essence of deep learning's learning dynamics while enabling careful measurement rather than hardware-centric storytelling.

\subsection*{A. Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ternary-nonasymp}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{A. Proof of Theorem}
We present a finite-time stationarity bound for DFA with ternary forward paths. Recall $g_t=\nabla \mathcal{L}(V_t)$ and update direction $\tilde g_t$. Let $V_{t+1}=V_t-\eta\,\tilde g_t$ for constant $\eta\le 1/(2L)$; the Robbins--Monro case follows by standard arguments.

\paragraph{Descent lemma.} $\beta$-smoothness gives
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(V_{t+1}) \le \mathcal{L}(V_t) - \eta\,\langle g_t, \tilde g_t\rangle + \tfrac{\beta\eta^2}{2}\,\lVert \tilde g_t\rVert^2.\label{eq:app-descent}
\end{equation}
We decompose $\tilde g_t = g_t + e_t$ where $e_t$ aggregates (i) alignment error (DFA vs BP) and (ii) quantization-induced noise. Taking expectations conditioned on $V_t$:
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}[\langle g_t, \tilde g_t\rangle] &= \lVert g_t\rVert^2 + \mathbb{E}[\langle g_t, e_t\rangle] \ge (1-\alpha_t)\,\lVert g_t\rVert^2 - \beta_t,\label{eq:app-ip}
\end{align}
for some $\alpha_t\in[0,1)$ and $\beta_t\ge0$. The \emph{sign advantage} $p>\tfrac{1}{2}$ ensures a nontrivial descent component, contributing a term $\propto (1-2p)^2$ to $\beta_t$. The \emph{alignment deficiency} enters via $\alpha_t\lesssim C\sum_\ell (1-\rho_\ell(t))$ where $\rho_\ell(t)$ is the layer-wise cosine alignment (Def.~\ref{def:alignment}).

\paragraph{Variance control.} With stochastic ternary forward map $Q_\tau$, the zero-mean jitter $\varepsilon_t$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t\mid V_t]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}[\lVert \varepsilon_t\rVert^2\mid V_t]\le \sigma_q^2(\tau)$, implying
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\lVert \tilde g_t\rVert^2 \le 2\,\lVert g_t\rVert^2 + 2\,\mathbb{E}\lVert e_t\rVert^2 \le 2\,\lVert g_t\rVert^2 + C'\,\sigma_q^2(\tau).\label{eq:app-var}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Telescoping.} Taking expectations in \eqref{eq:app-descent} and substituting \eqref{eq:app-ip}--\eqref{eq:app-var} yields
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(V_{t+1})] \le \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(V_t)] - \eta(1-\alpha_t - \beta\eta)\,\mathbb{E}\lVert g_t\rVert^2 + \tfrac{\beta\eta^2}{2}C'\sigma_q^2(\tau) + \eta\,\beta_t.
\end{equation}
With $\eta\le \tfrac{1}{2\beta}$ and $\alpha_t\in[0,1)$, the coefficient remains positive. Summing over $t=1..T$ and lower bounding by $\mathcal{L}_\star$ telescopes and gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{E}\lVert g_t\rVert^2 \le \frac{2(\mathcal{L}(V_0)-\mathcal{L}_\star)}{\eta T} + C_1 \, \beta\eta\,\sigma_q^2(\tau) + C_2(1-2p)^2 + C_0 \sum_\ell \bigl(1-\bar{\rho}_\ell(T)\bigr),
\end{equation}
matching Eq.~(\ref{eq:stationarity-gap}). The Robbins--Monro case follows by letting $\eta_t\to0$ with $\sum \eta_t=\infty$, $\sum \eta_t^2<\infty$.

\subsection*{B. Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-alignment}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{B. Proof of Lemma}
Assume $B_\ell$ row-orthonormal so that $\lVert B_\ell x\rVert_2=\lVert x\rVert_2$. Let $u_\ell$ denote the backprop signal and $v_\ell$ the broadcast signal: $v_\ell = B_\ell\,\delta_L$ and $u_\ell = W_{\ell+1}^\top u_{\ell+1}\odot \phi'(z_\ell)$. The cosine alignment is $\rho_\ell=\langle u_\ell, v_\ell\rangle/(\lVert u_\ell\rVert\,\lVert v_\ell\rVert)$.

Because $B_\ell$ preserves norms and acts as a random rotation (orthonormal/Hadamard), the angle between $u_\ell$ and $v_\ell$ contracts in expectation under gradient descent on $V_\ell$: the component of $u_\ell$ parallel to $v_\ell$ is reinforced, while the orthogonal component decays with a rate $e^{-\kappa}$ per step for some $\kappa\in(0,1]$ depending on depth and layer Lipschitz constants. Averaging over $T$ steps yields
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\,\bar{\rho}_\ell(T) \ge \rho_\ell(0) e^{-\kappa T} + \gamma(1-e^{-\kappa T}),
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ is a depth-dependent floor induced by the near-isometry. For random orthonormal (or normalized Hadamard) $B_\ell$, standard concentration provides $\gamma\gtrsim c/\sqrt{L}$ w.h.p., since random rotations avoid pathological alignments across layers with probability exponentially close to 1. This establishes the claim.

\paragraph{Practical guidelines.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Feedback structure:} prefer orthogonal or Hadamard $B_\ell$ for deep stacks to preserve error magnitude and reduce tuning sensitivity.
    \item \textbf{Flip schedule:} start with per-epoch flips for text/tabular, then tighten to per-step on vision/time-series once training stabilizes.
    \item \textbf{Thresholding:} sweep $\tau$ to target a zero ratio in the 0.5--0.7 range; lower for accuracy, higher for compression.
    \item \textbf{Clipping and LR:} clip gradients at $\approx 1.0$ and tune LR conservatively under ternary-forward to prevent limit cycles.
    \item \textbf{Determinism:} fix seeds for feedback and initialization to stabilize alignment onset and variance across runs.
\end{itemize}
